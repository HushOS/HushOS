# Companies: what the company release adds to the group core

**Status:** a design, written on 10 November 2026 and revised five times the same day: after an independent review (18 findings), after the owner's direction that the server keeps no readable activity history, and after a second (13 findings), a third (8) and a fourth (5) review round. All are applied; section 9 says where a revision differs from the reviewer's recommendation. The fourth round found no break in confidentiality or integrity and called the document ready to commit once its findings were in. Of section 8's plan, PR 1 is built (roster format 3 and the company key, in `packages/crypto` and `crates/hushos-core`, with invariants I17 to I30 in `groups-invariants.md`) and so is PR 2 (area lists, heads and the resolver, I31 to I37); nothing else is, and no app or server code calls either. The server still refuses `org` groups (`packages/groups/src/server.ts`, 409 `unsupported`).

This document specifies the company core: a company as a group, who opens which folder, the key the company holds, joining, offboarding, the admin browser, the activity ledger, alerts, policies and spaces. It builds on `docs/groups-design.md` (the group core), `docs/groups-server.md` (the server contract), `docs/optional-verification.md` (first use, and checking later) and `docs/groups-threat-model.md`, whose section 8 attacks this design.

It follows the build plan ("HushOS for Companies — Build Plan", 31 decisions agreed on 14 October) as the owner changed it on 10 November 2026:

- joining by company email domain can be automatic, as a policy a company turns on, off by default;
- single sign-on, custom domains and white-label are not in this release;
- up to 50 seats self-serve, raised per company by an operator;
- the activity record is an end-to-end encrypted ledger that members' apps write, and **the server keeps no readable history of it**: what it records itself it seals to the company's key as it writes;
- device approval is level 1 only, never on without a policy or the person's own choice, with an admin's one-time waiver;
- "team" means the company; folders have people lists, named sub-teams come later;
- child accounts are built after companies, so nothing a guardian requires is in this document.

Where the cryptography does not support a direction as given, section 9 says so and section 10 asks.

Plans, seat billing and affiliates, and own bucket, region, retention and exports, are not in this document (section 6).

## 1. Model

### 1.1 What is reused, and what is new

Reused unchanged from the group core:

- the group key (Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65), its seals and successions (`groups-design.md` 2.2, 2.3, 2.5);
- the roster chain, its acceptance, marks, the hard warning, the guard and the cache (2.4, 2.7);
- area keys listed on the roster by commitment, root commitments and retired areas (2.6, 2.10);
- area grants and their opening rules (2.6);
- first use and checking later with a pair code (`optional-verification.md`);
- invitations, acceptance, admission, holds, departures, `not-member` (`groups-server.md`);
- re-keyed records, which keep each writer's signature through a key change (`integrity-design.md`).

The crypto core already parses `org` rosters and the roles `data_admin` and `recovery` (`ORG_ROLES` in `packages/crypto/src/groups.ts`), and already leaves companies out of two family rules: a removal need not rotate every area, and the 1,024-area cap (`group-trust.ts`, `next.kind === 'family'`). The schema already has both kinds and all five roles (`packages/db/src/schema/groups.ts`). The shared fixtures (`web-groups.json`, `core-groups.json`) hold `org` rosters at formats 1 and 2; PR 1 regenerates them at format 3, since no server ever stored an `org` group.

New in this document:

- roster format 3: the company key and kit on the roster, an area's kind and its list's digest on its row, the auto-join mark, the heads digest, the Owner rule and the recovery roster (2.1);
- the company (custody) key, its seals, the kit, its proof and the group key's escrow (2.2);
- area lists (2.3);
- share, link and stop records and the share log (2.4);
- activity records and the sealed ledger (2.5);
- everything in section 3 from 3.2 on.

### 1.2 Roles

| Capability   | What it is                  | What it can do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `members`    | Holds the group signing key | Signs rosters: invites, lets people in, removes Members and Billing holders, makes an Admin, creates folders, changes folder keys, turns auto-join on or off. Removing or demoting an Admin, and anything about an Owner or the kit, is an Owner's (2.1, rules 8 and 12) |
| `data_admin` | Holds the company key       | Opens every company folder (the admin browser), reads the activity ledger                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `billing`    | Nothing cryptographic       | Plan, seats, invoices                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `recovery`   | May use the recovery kit    | With the kit, after the wait: becomes an Owner (2.1, the recovery roster)                                                                                                                                                                                                |

The apps offer four roles, each a fixed set:

| Role    | Capabilities            | For                                                                 |
| ------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owner   | all four                | Whoever runs the company. At least one, always.                     |
| Admin   | `members`, `data_admin` | People who manage people and can open company files                 |
| Billing | `billing`               | Finance staff. They hold neither the company key nor the group key. |
| Member  | none                    | Everyone else                                                       |

A Billing holder or a Member opens their own company area and the folders they are on, like anyone. What they never hold is a key that opens other people's.

`recovery` can also be given to a Member or Billing holder on its own ("Can use the recovery kit"), so the person who keeps the kit need not be an Admin day to day. It is a latent Owner: with the kit, that person becomes one.

**Why no people-only manager.** The plan had the Owner hold `members` alone by default. That does not hold: the person who signs a folder's key change supplies the new key (the roster row is computed from it, `nextRoster`), and the person who lets someone in creates their folder. A `members` holder without `data_admin` would still have made, and could keep, every key they touched. So `members` is always paired with `data_admin`, and the role that cannot read other people's files is Billing. Section 9.1 has the detail and the alternative.

**Owners rank above Admins.** Nothing in the family core ranks one `members` holder above another, so a single stolen Admin session could remove every other Admin and Owner and strip `recovery` from everyone, leaving the kit unusable. Format 3 adds a chain rule (2.1, rule 8): any change to the entry of an Owner or of someone who holds `recovery` (their keys included), giving or taking `recovery`, and a new kit, are an Owner's to sign. And only an Owner signs a new group key or a new company key (rule 12), so only an Owner removes or demotes an Admin. An Admin still lets people in, removes and changes Members and Billing holders, makes another Admin under the two-person rule (2.2), creates folders and changes their keys.

**Leaving with a role is by notice.** Marking an Owner, an Admin or a kit holder left is an Owner's roster (rules 8 and 12), and nobody can sign the roster that marks themselves left together with the keys it makes. So all three leave the same way: they give 7 days' notice, are paused at once, and are out after 7 days whatever anyone else does (3.6). The last Owner makes someone else an Owner first.

An Owner who wants to stay in the company without the role steps down to Admin on their own roster, with the new kit and company key that taking `recovery` costs. Down to Member or Billing is another Owner's roster: it drops `members`, the succession that follows must be signed by someone who is an Owner on that roster (rule 12), and the person stepping down no longer is.

Every roster keeps at least one active Owner. `nextRoster` and acceptance refuse one that does not (`checkRosterOrg`, rule 4), which also keeps a `billing` and a `members` holder; the family core checks the `billing` holder only on the server, and for `org` it is the chain's.

Managers are checked before they get their role, as in families (`optional-verification.md` 3.6): giving anyone `members`, `data_admin` or `recovery` needs a verified pin of them (twelve words, once). Folding a new identity into an Owner's or a kit holder's entry, when another Owner does it, needs the check again, of the new keys (2.2).

Owners, Admins and `recovery` holders must have the second step on their account (the plan's Layer 5: someone who can open every company file, or become someone who can, cannot be password-only). The server refuses to create a company, and refuses a role change that gives `members`, `data_admin` or `recovery`, for an account without it (409 `two-step-required`), once two-step sign-in is released. The requirement ends when the membership or the role does; the person's step stays on until they turn it off themselves.

### 1.3 Areas

A storage area is a workspace row, as in families. A company has two kinds:

| Kind       | What it is                                   | Who is on its list                                                                                           | Made                                                   |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `shared`   | A company folder: "Clients", "Finance"       | People an Admin or the folder's managers chose, each a manager, editor or viewer; or everyone in the company | By an Admin                                            |
| `employee` | One person's company files ("My Acme files") | Its assignee alone, as manager                                                                               | By the Admin who lets them in, in the admitting roster |

- An employee area belongs to the company. It stays when its assignee leaves, and the admin browser opens it. A person's personal space is a different workspace, owned by them, and nothing in this document reaches it (2.6).
- An employee area's list is its assignee and nobody else. An addition to it is refused (`invalid` (`list-employee`)); handing it to someone else is a new base list on a roster (3.6).
- A member shares single items from any folder they can edit through share records (2.4). That is how ad-hoc sharing between colleagues works without an Admin.
- Members do not create top-level shared folders in this release. They ask an Admin, or share a folder from their own area (decision 10.4).
- Moving an item between areas, or between spaces, is copy then trash, every version, as in families (`envelope-migrations.md`). A node is never re-parented across workspaces.
- No private child under a shared parent: the apps do not offer it and the server refuses it. A folder's list covers its whole tree.
- Download URLs for a company area live 15 minutes, as share URLs do (`SHARE_URL_TTL_SECONDS`), not the hour a personal space gets, so the longest a removed person keeps fetching is 15 minutes.

## 2. Cryptography

Encodings are `groups-design.md` 2.1's, unchanged: canonical JSON arrays, lowercase UUIDs, base64url without padding, lists sorted with no duplicates, verifiers that re-encode. Every statement below ships in `packages/crypto` and `crates/hushos-core` with shared fixtures.

"Sealed with suite 4" means the construction of `identity-pq-design.md` 3 as key seals and area grants use it: an ephemeral X25519 key and an ML-KEM-768 encapsulation to the recipient's two public keys, XChaCha20-Poly1305 with the statement (without its final digest) as associated data, and the sealer's hybrid identity signature (3,373 bytes) over the statement. A secret of n bytes sealed this way is 32 + 1,088 + 24 + n + 16 + 3,373 bytes (`hybridSealBytes`): 4,565 for 32 bytes, as an area grant is.

### 2.1 Roster format 3

A company's rosters are format 3 from version 1. A family's stay format 2.

```
["hushos/group/roster",3,groupId,"org",rosterVersion,previousRosterDigest,groupKeyVersion,signerUserId,members,areas,custody,rules,auto,heads]
members = as formats 1 and 2 | ["same",membersDigest]
areas   = [[workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,"key",commitment,rootCommitment,areaKind,listDigest] | [workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,"retired"], ...]
custody = [custodyKeyVersion,custodyEncryptionPublicKey,custodyKemPublicKeyDigest,kitVersion,kitEncryptionPublicKey,kitKemPublicKeyDigest,kitSigningPublicKey,kitMlDsaPublicKeyDigest]
rules   = [autoJoin,autoJoinDailyLimit]        autoJoin = 0 | 1; the limit 1 to 50
auto    = [] | [utcDay,countThatDay]            utcDay = "YYYY-MM-DD"; the count 1 to 50
heads   = [areaCount,headsDigest]
```

- `members` may be `["same",membersDigest]` on a roster that changes no entry (a new folder, a batch of key changes, a new kit, or nothing but `heads`): `membersDigest` is SHA-256 of the canonical `members` array of the roster before, and acceptance puts that array in its place. Never at version 1. A company signs many rosters that touch no person, and each full list is about 40 KB at 50 seats, which a new device downloads from roster 1 (3.11, decision 10.21).
- `rules` holds the one policy that makes members' devices act on their own: auto-join (3.2). It is on the roster so that only an Admin's signature turns it on, and so that the chain's marks stop a server replaying an old "on". Every other policy is a server rule and stays a server row (3.5, 3.9).
- `auto` marks a roster that an Admin's device signed unattended under auto-join (rule 9).
- `heads` commits to every folder's list and share log as the signer's device verified them (rule 10).
- `areaKind` is `"shared"` or `"employee"`. It is fixed at the area's first row; a later row with another kind is refused.
- `listDigest` is SHA-256 of the area list statement (2.3) that is the folder's list at that key version, its **base list**.
- `custody` names the company key and the kit by their X25519 public keys and the digests of their ML-KEM-768 public keys, and the kit's signing keys by its Ed25519 public key and the digest of its ML-DSA-65 public key. The server serves the lattice keys and a reader checks them by digest, as it does members' keys.
- The statement's version is the format, so the signatures cover it. Format 3 is accepted only for kind `org`, and kind `org` only at format 3. A 14-part statement cannot parse as format 1 or 2.
- A signed roster is `{ statement, signature, signerSignature }` as in families. A recovery roster (rule 11) carries a third signature beside them, `kitSignature`.

**What acceptance adds for format 3** (`checkRosterOrg`, Rust `check_roster_org`, called from the same place `checkAreas` is):

1. **The company key moves forward only.** `custodyKeyVersion` is the previous roster's, or one more. One more means both keys are new: never the keys of an earlier version. The same for `kitVersion` and the kit's four keys. While a version stays, its keys stay: a roster that keeps `kitVersion` carries the same four kit keys, and one that keeps `custodyKeyVersion` the same two. Version 1 of each is on roster 1.
2. **Dropping a data admin replaces the company key.** A roster on which an entry that held `data_admin` on the roster before no longer holds it, or is marked `left`, must raise `custodyKeyVersion` (`A roster that removes a data admin replaces the company key.`). `nextRoster` refuses to build it otherwise.
3. **Roles.** An entry's roles are one of six sets and no other: none; `billing`; `recovery`; `billing` and `recovery`; `members` and `data_admin`; all four (1.2). So `members` and `data_admin` come together or not at all. `guardian` is refused in an `org` roster, though `ORG_ROLES` parses it today. **An entry marked `left` has no roles:** the roster that marks it left empties them, and a `left` entry that names a role is refused. So "holds `recovery`", "holds `data_admin`" and "an Owner", in every rule here, mean an **active** entry with those roles. (Otherwise a `left` entry that kept `recovery` would set rule 7 off again on every later roster.)
4. **An Owner stays.** At least one active entry holds all four.
5. **Removals do not rotate in the same statement.** As today for `org`: the roster that marks someone `left` need not carry their folders' rows. Those key changes are owed (3.6) and each lands on a later roster.
6. **Rows per roster.** At most 8 key rows that rotate an existing area (`MAX_ORG_ROTATIONS_PER_ROSTER`), so a roster call's body stays bounded (3.11). New areas and tombstones are not counted.
7. **The kit follows `recovery`.** A roster on which an entry that held `recovery` on the roster before no longer holds it, or is marked `left`, must raise `kitVersion`. A roster that raises `kitVersion` must raise `custodyKeyVersion` (`A new recovery kit needs a new company key.`). The reasons are in 2.2: whoever made or kept a kit knows its secret, and the old kit's seal of the current company key cannot be taken back.
8. **Owners rank above Admins.** Call an entry **guarded** when it holds `recovery` on the roster before or on this one. Every Owner's is. A roster is signed by an entry that was an Owner on the roster before (`Only an owner can change an owner, the recovery kit or its holders.`) when any of these is true:
    - a guarded entry differs from the roster before in **any** field: its keys, its key version, its roles or its status;
    - a guarded entry is new on this roster;
    - `kitVersion` rises.

    And for every entry that holds `members` on the roster before or on this one, guarded or not: **a change to its keys or key version is accepted only on that member's own roster** (their fold-in, signed under the keys the entry named before, as every roster is) **or on a roster an Owner signs** (`Only an owner, or the member themselves, changes the keys of someone who signs member lists.`). So an Admin cannot write keys into a fellow Admin's entry, and cannot write keys into a Member's entry on the roster that makes that Member an Admin.

    So an Admin cannot remove or demote an Owner, cannot give or take `recovery`, cannot fold a new identity into an Owner's or a kit holder's entry, cannot write keys of their own into one, and cannot swap the kit. The second draft covered roles and status only. Entry keys are a holder's claim in the family core, so an Admin could have rewritten an Owner's keys to keys of their own and then signed "as the Owner"; and nothing said who may raise `kitVersion`, so an Admin could have put a kit of their own making on the roster, which rule 7 would never have retired.

    An Owner may sign a change to their own entry. Folding in their own new identity is signed under the keys their entry named on the roster before, as every roster is (`signedByMember`): an account keeps its earlier identities' private keys after a signed replacement. The recovery roster (rule 11) is accepted under its own rule.

9. **An automatic admission says so.** A roster with `auto` set must: stand on a roster whose `rules` have `autoJoin` 1; add exactly one new entry, active, with no roles, and change nothing else in `members`; carry `utcDay` not earlier than the `utcDay` of the latest earlier roster with `auto` set; **change nothing else at all: the same `rules` and `custody` as the roster before, and no area row but one new `employee` area (the newcomer's own);** and carry `countThatDay` 1 if that day is later, otherwise that roster's count plus one, never above the `autoJoinDailyLimit` of the roster it stands on. A roster without `auto` may not have been signed unattended; nothing in the chain can check that, and the residual is in 3.2. **A roster without `auto` that turns `autoJoin` from 0 to 1 starts the count afresh:** the next automatic roster counts from 1 on any day. Without that, one automatic admission dated far ahead (a device with a wrong clock, or a hostile one) would have stopped every later one for good, since no honest day is "not earlier". The day is the signing device's own word, so the chain's count bounds a day's admissions only as far as the day is honest: a signer who advances the day restarts the count. **The server refuses an `auto` roster whose `utcDay` is not its own UTC day, give or take one** (`invalid` (`auto-day`)); that is a **server rule**, and it is what makes the daily limit a limit against a signer's device. The kit's signature belongs to the recovery roster alone: a roster that is not one and carries a `kitSignature` is refused (`Only a recovery roster carries the recovery kit's signature.`).
10. **Heads.** `areaCount` is the number of live areas after this roster. A device takes a roster as accepted with its statement alone; what it does with `heads` is 2.3's and 2.4's.
11. **The recovery roster.** A roster whose signer holds `recovery` but not `members` on the roster before is accepted only when all of these hold:
    - it changes exactly one entry, the signer's own, and only by giving it all four roles. Its keys do not change;
    - it carries no area row, an empty `auto`, and the same `custody`, `rules` and `heads` fields as the roster before;
    - it holds under the group key, and under the signer's identity keys as their entry named them on the roster before, like any roster;
    - **it carries the kit's signature.** `kitSignature` is a hybrid signature (Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65, 3,373 bytes, with the ML-DSA context `hushos/group/recovery-kit/v1`, the kit's own and no other key's) over

        ```
        ["hushos/group/recovery-roster",1,groupId,rosterVersion,kitVersion,sha256(rosterStatement)]
        ```

        and verifies under the kit's signing keys in the `custody` field of the roster before, the ML-DSA key matched by its digest. It is stored with the roster and served with it.

    Acceptance records it (`AcceptedRosters.recoveries`: the version and the signer), and every member's app says so once: "Sam used Acme's recovery kit and is now an owner."

12. **New keys are an Owner's.** A roster that raises `custodyKeyVersion` is signed by an entry that was an Owner on the roster before (`Only an owner can replace the company's keys.`). A group key succession of an `org` group is accepted only when its signer is an Owner on the roster it is tied to (`checkNextSuccession`, in its `org` branch; a family's stays any `members` holder).

    Rules 2 and 3 together mean that a roster on which anyone stops holding `members` raises `custodyKeyVersion`. So the roster that removes or demotes an Admin, or marks a departed one left, is an Owner's, and so is the succession it makes due. A roster that only gives roles (making an Admin) raises neither and stays an Admin's. The recovery roster raises neither.

    Why. Rule 8 guarded entries and the kit, and left the keys themselves to any Admin. An Admin who removed another Admin signed the succession and the company key's replacement, and only the server checked that the new seeds were sealed to the Owners. With the server's help that Admin could leave those seals out and seal junk to the kit. The Owners would stay on the roster and never be able to sign again, even after the server turned honest. Now nobody below an Owner makes a key the company runs on. What is left is between Owners, who are peers (2.2, "A holder without a seal").

The other rules of `groups-design.md` 2.4 hold as written: the signer active on the roster they sign, no admission on the roster that drops a `members` holder, a succession due when one is dropped.

**Why the recovery roster is in the core, and why it is sound.** Without it, a company whose only Owner loses their account can never have an Owner again, and the kit would open files nobody is allowed to fetch. With it, anyone the company gave `recovery`, holding the kit, restores management.

- **It needs the kit, and that is a fact of the chain.** The kit's signing keys are on every roster, under the group signature. Every device refuses a recovery roster that lacks the kit's signature. The second draft rested "needs the kit" on who could hold the group seed ("only someone who holds the current group seed through the kit and the escrow"). That was false. Every Admin holds the group seed. An Admin working with a `recovery` holder, or an Admin who wrote keys of their own into a `recovery` holder's entry, could have signed a recovery roster with no kit at all.
- **What an Admin with the group seed can sign:** any roster that touches no guarded entry, no other signer's keys, no kit and no key the company runs on. That is letting in, removing and changing Members and Billing holders; making a Member an Admin; folders and their keys; auto-join. **What they cannot sign:** anything rule 8 names; a roster that takes `members` from anyone or replaces the company key, and a group key succession (rule 12); or a recovery roster. Against an Owner's entry an Admin has no more power than a Member.
- **Against another Admin's entry, the same holds for keys** (rule 8, since the review of PR 1). An earlier draft left an Admin's entry unguarded and said that planting keys in a peer's entry "gains them no key or role an Admin lacks". That was wrong: an Admin who planted keys of their own in a fellow Admin's entry, and was then removed, would have been sealed the next company key and the next group key as that fellow Admin, by an Owner's device sealing to the entry's keys. Now the keys of anyone who holds `members` change only on that person's own roster or an Owner's. What an Admin can still do to a fellow Admin is what the roles allow: nothing about their roles or status either (taking `members` away raises `custodyKeyVersion`, rule 12). And a **Member's** entry is still a holder's claim, as in a family: an Admin can write other keys into it. That gains nothing by itself (a Member holds no key of the company's), and the roster that would then make that entry an Admin or a kit holder is built only with the signer's fresh words check of exactly the keys the entry names (`nextRoster`'s `checked`, 2.2): a device rule, the same one a guarded fold-in needs.
- **What rule 8 still leaves: plant, then promote, in two rosters.** An Admin with the server's help can write keys of their own into a Member's entry on one roster and make that Member an Admin on the next. Rule 8 refuses both on one roster; across two, each roster is one the chain allows an Admin (a Member's keys are a holder's claim; an Admin may make an Admin). The check before a promotion is a device rule, and the device that would run it is the planting Admin's own, modified, so it does not. The result is an Admin's entry whose keys the planter holds, sealed the company key and the group key under the real Member's name.
    - **It is not hidden.** Every device sees the promotion on the roster ("Dana became an admin, added by Maya"), signed by the Admin who made it, and the real Member's device finds keys that are not its own in its own entry and says so (`ownEntryWrongAt`).
    - **It is no more than the role already allows.** An Admin may make another Admin (2.2), so an Admin who wants a second Admin's keys under their control can promote an account they control without planting anything. What the planting adds is the wrong name on it: the roster says the new Admin is a colleague who is not.
    - **Closing it on the chain** means only an Owner makes someone an Admin. That is decision 56 of section 10, the owner's to take; the rule is unchanged here.
- **What a `recovery` holder with the kit can sign:** one roster, their own promotion. Not someone else's, because the signer's identity signature must hold under their own entry. The kit in the hands of someone with no `recovery` entry signs nothing a device accepts.
- **It no longer leans on the succession rule.** An Owner demoted to "can use the kit" was a `members` holder dropped, so the group key was replaced on that roster (`HOLDER_LEFT` and `REPLACED_KEY` in `rosterRules`), and the kit is their only way back to the current one. That stays true, and `groups-invariants.md` records it as an invariant in PR 1. But if it were ever relaxed for `org`, the kit's signature would still be required.
- It is replay-safe by position: it is a roster at one version, on one previous digest, and the kit's signature covers both.
- It makes an Owner, not an Admin. The person it replaces is an Owner whose account is lost; rule 8 lets only an Owner remove them, and anything less would leave nobody able to.
- **Its timing is the server's, and only the server's.** A `recovery` holder with the kit and the server's help needs no wait. The 48 hours, the objection and the seven-day hold (2.2) are server rules. What the chain adds is that it cannot be done unseen: the roster is on every device, and every member's app says who used the kit.

### 2.2 The company key

**The key.** An X25519 key pair and an ML-KEM-768 key pair, made on the creator's device from a 96-byte seed (32 bytes for X25519, 64 for ML-KEM key generation). It encrypts only. It never signs.

**Pinning.** Members' devices learn it from the roster's `custody` field, so it is covered by the group signature, the chain and every mark the person holds. A device seals to the company key at the latest version on its accepted chain and to no other. A server cannot swap it without forging a roster.

**Fingerprint.** For the creator and the kit only, twelve words from:

```
SHA-256("hushos/group/custody/fingerprint/v1" ‖ groupId (36 ASCII bytes) ‖ u64be(custodyKeyVersion) ‖ custodyEncryptionPublicKey (32) ‖ custodyKemPublicKey (1184))
```

The kit prints them, so a person restoring from a kit can see it is this company's.

**Folder keys sealed to it.** Every area key is sealed to the company key with suite 4:

```
["hushos/group/custody-seal",1,groupId,workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,custodyKeyVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]
```

- HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/custody-seal/v1`.
- The sealer of a seal that rides on a roster is that roster's signer, who made the key. The sealer of a re-seal after a company key replacement (route C8) is any entry active with `data_admin` on the roster the seal names.
- A roster call that creates or rotates an area carries the seal, or is refused (`invalid` (`custody-seal`)), as it is refused without the grants it owes.
- **Checking a seal.** The server cannot tell whether a seal opens. A data admin's device can: it opens each seal it has not checked and compares the key's commitment with the one the roster lists for that area and version. A seal that does not open, or opens to another key, is shown on the company's page ("Maya's key for Clients can't be opened by the company. Change that folder's keys.") and names its sealer under their own signature. Data admins' devices check new seals whenever they sync.

**The seed, sealed to data admins.**

```
["hushos/group/custody-key-seal",1,groupId,custodyKeyVersion,holderUserId,holderKeyVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]
```

- HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/custody-key-seal/v1`. Suite 4, to the holder's identity. 4,629 bytes.
- The sealer is an active `data_admin` (the creator, for version 1). The holder is active with `data_admin` on the roster the seal names, at the exact key version their entry names.
- An opener derives the public keys from the seed and compares them with the chain's at that version, as a holder does with the group seed.
- A roster that newly gives someone `data_admin` carries their seal, or is refused (`custody-holder-seal`).
- **A data admin's identity changes.** After a signed key replacement is folded into their entry, another data admin (or their own device, which still holds the seed) re-seals the seed to the new identity on route C30, as route 9 does for the group seed; the old seal is deleted. An **unsigned** change (they started over with new keys) is treated as a removal: an Owner's device (rule 12) removes and re-admits the role with the company key replaced, because the keys that opened the old seal are no longer vouched for. That is the full price of removing a data admin, every folder's key change included, for one person pressing "Start over with new keys" (decision 10.35). Keys a person says were exposed are handled the same way, by choice ("Replace the company key").
- **An Owner's or a kit holder's identity changes.** A signed replacement is folded in by an Owner: themselves, with their earlier keys, or another Owner (2.1, rule 8).
    - **Another Owner checks first.** Their device folds a new identity into a guarded entry only after a fresh words check of the new keys: a verified pin made after the replacement, as giving the role needs (1.2). A signed link is not enough here. Someone who stole an Owner's old keys can sign a link from them to keys of their own, and with the server's help have it served to another Owner's device, which `foldInEntry` would otherwise follow. It is a device rule (`nextRoster` takes the check as an input for a guarded entry and refuses without it); the chain cannot see a words check. The same input covers **giving `members` or `recovery` to anyone**, already on the roster or let in with the role: `nextRoster` refuses to build that roster without a verified pin, the signer's own, of exactly the keys the entry names. A Member or a Billing holder is let in without one. So keys planted in a Member's entry cannot be promoted on the roster's word, and the three clients do not each re-implement the check of 1.2.
    - **Exposed keys.** Until an Owner's entry is put right or removed, whoever holds its keys and a copy of that Owner's seals is that Owner. An Owner who says their keys were seen is removed and let in again by another Owner, after a check, with the new kit and keys that costs.
    - An unsigned change (they started over with new keys) can be put right only by another Owner, who removes and re-admits them after a check, with the new kit and company key that taking `recovery` costs.
    - **"Start over with new keys" is never refused,** to an Owner or to anyone (`devices-design.md` 3, once it is built). People press it when their keys are already gone, and a refusal would lock a person out of their own account to protect a role they can no longer use. It warns first. To an Owner: "You are an owner of Acme. After this you can't manage Acme until another owner lets you back in." To the only Owner: "Acme will have no owner. Make someone else an owner first, or make sure someone can use the recovery kit." The entry is then a lost Owner, which the design has to survive anyway, and which the recovery roster is for (decision 10.46). The third draft refused it to the only Owner, which protected nothing.

**The seed, sealed to the kit.**

```
["hushos/group/kit-seal",1,groupId,custodyKeyVersion,kitVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]
```

- HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/kit-seal/v1`. Suite 4, to the kit's public keys. 4,629 bytes.
- Because the kit is a key pair, a device replaces the company key and seals the new seed to the kit without the kit being present.

**Proving a kit seal.** Nobody can open a kit seal without the kit, so a bug, or a hostile sealer, could seal junk to it and nobody would know until the day of recovery. So the sealer also stores what makes the seal reproducible:

```
["hushos/group/kit-seal-proof",1,groupId,custodyKeyVersion,kitVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(kitSeal),sha256(sealed)]
sealed = ephemeralX25519Secret (32) ‖ kemEncapsulationRandomness (32) ‖ nonce (24)
```

- HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/kit-seal-proof/v1`. Suite 4, to the company key at `custodyKeyVersion`. 4,621 bytes.
- A data admin's device opens the proof with the company seed, runs the kit seal again with those three values and the seed it already holds as the plaintext, and compares the first 1,256 bytes (everything before the signature) with the stored kit seal. A mismatch is shown like a bad folder seal, naming the sealer, and the fix is a new kit.
- Suite 4 sealing gains an entry that takes the three values instead of drawing them (`sealWithCoins`, Rust `seal_with_coins`), used for this proof and by tests only. ML-KEM-768 encapsulation with chosen randomness is in FIPS 203, and both libraries have it: noble's `encapsulate(publicKey, msg)`, and the Rust core's `encapsulate_deterministic`, which `identity.rs` already calls.
- The proof gives a data admin nothing: they hold the seed it protects. It gives the kit's holder nothing they lack.
- A roster call that raises `custodyKeyVersion` or `kitVersion` carries the kit seal and its proof, or is refused (`kit-seal`).

**The kit.** A 32-byte secret, shown as 24 words and saved as a file. The kit's key pairs derive from it:

```
kitSeed (160) = HKDF-SHA-256(ikm = kitSecret, salt = 32 zero bytes, info = "hushos/group/recovery-kit/v1" ‖ groupId, length = 160)
              = X25519 seed (32) ‖ ML-KEM-768 seed (64) ‖ Ed25519 seed (32) ‖ ML-DSA-65 seed (32)
```

- The first two open kit seals. The last two sign one thing only: a recovery roster (2.1, rule 11). All four public keys are in the roster's `custody` field.
- It is made on the creator's device and confirmed (the person types four of its words back) before `POST /api/groups` is sent. Nothing of the company exists on the server until then.
- The file holds the secret, the company's name and id, the kit version, the date and the company key's twelve words. The server never sees the secret.
- **A kit is a full key, and it cannot be taken back alone.** Whoever made a kit, or was handed it, knows its secret. The server stores the current company key sealed to it. So an old kit, with a copy of the server's database, opens the company key for as long as that company key is current, and through it every folder. That is why rule 7 ties them: when anyone who could hold the kit loses `recovery` or leaves, the roster carries a new kit **and** a new company key, and every folder's key change becomes owed, as for a removed data admin.
- An Owner makes a new kit at any time (route C9). It costs the same: a new company key and every folder's key change. The screen says so, and says when it is worth it: the kit was used, lost, or may have been seen.

**The group key's escrow.** So that the kit can restore management as well as files, the group seed is sealed to the company key:

```
["hushos/group/key-escrow",1,groupId,groupKeyVersion,custodyKeyVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]
```

- HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/key-escrow/v1`. Suite 4, to the company key. 4,597 bytes.
- Made at creation, at every group key succession and at every company key replacement, by the signer of that call. A call that makes either without it is refused (`key-escrow`). A device takes an escrow only from that signer, and only where they held `data_admin` on the roster the escrow names: an escrow "by" an Owner that names a roster after they left, signed with keys someone wrote into the entry they left, is refused and says nothing of them.
- It gives data admins nothing new: they hold `members`, and so the seed, already (1.2).

**Replacing the company key.** On the roster that raises `custodyKeyVersion`, signed by an Owner (2.1, rule 12), in one call:

1. the new public keys in the roster's `custody` field;
2. the new seed sealed to every remaining data admin and to the kit, with the kit seal's proof;
3. the group key's escrow under the new key;
4. `previousEnvelope`: the old seed sealed to the new company key with suite 4 (`["hushos/group/custody-previous",1,groupId,custodyKeyVersion,previousVersion,sealerUserId,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]`, `info` `hushos/group/custody-previous/v1`), so later data admins still open earlier seals and the earlier ledger;
5. every live area's current key sealed to the new company key, in batches after the call (route C8). Until an area's seal lands, the admin browser opens it through the previous key.

**Whose signature a seal is checked under.** A seal to the company key or the kit names the roster its sealer had accepted. It is checked under the signing keys the sealer's entry named **on that roster**, which every device keeps from the chain, never under the keys the entry names now: `previousEnvelope` and a folder's seal are not remade when their sealer's keys change, so judged by today's keys they would stop opening the day an Owner started over with new keys and was let back in. A check then says one of three things: the seal holds and its sealer's entry still names the keys that signed it; it holds and those keys have since been replaced (it still opens, and is nobody's word now); or it is bad, naming its sealer unless their keys have since been replaced. The seed sealed to a data admin is the exception: it is remade on a key change, and is checked under its sealer's current entry, as the group seed's seal is.

**Every data admin's device checks what it can open** (route C6, with the folder seals): the escrow (the seed's public keys are the chain's group keys at that version), `previousEnvelope` (the seed's public keys are the chain's company key at the previous version), and the kit seal by its proof. Each failure names its sealer.

**A holder without a seal.** A data admin's device that finds no seal of the current company key addressed to its person, or a `members` holder's that finds none of the current group key, says so at once: "This device wasn't given Acme's current keys. Ask an owner." It reports it (route C7, `missing`), and the server lists it for every Owner (`due.seals`). An honest replacement seals to every remaining holder, and the server refuses one that does not. That refusal is a **server rule**; this warning is what a holder sees when the signer and the server both failed them.

- **One Owner with the server's help can still leave another Owner out this way,** and the kit does not undo it. An Owner who does that would seal junk to the kit as well, and the one left out cannot tell: the kit seal's proof is sealed to the new company key, which is the key they were not given. So the kit is not a remedy here, and nothing in this design is. Owners are peers: there is no rank above them to appeal to. What the one left out has is the warning, at once, on their own device, and the roster, on which every member's device shows that the company's keys were replaced and who signed it.

**What replacing does not do.** A removed data admin held the old seed. It opened every folder key then current. Only changing those folder keys takes them out, so the server marks every live area's key change as owed in the same transaction, and the Admins' devices work through them (3.6). The company's page shows the count until it is zero. This is the cost of removing someone who could read everything, and the screen says so before the click.

**Using the kit.** A `recovery` holder asks (route C10) with a signed request:

```
["hushos/group/recovery-request",1,groupId,requesterUserId,requesterKeyVersion,kitVersion,custodyKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,requestedAt]
```

- It carries the requester's hybrid identity signature. The server verifies it under their roster entry, which must hold `recovery`, stores it, mails every Owner, notifies every Admin, and serves it with the chain to every member (route 5, `recoveryRequests`), whose apps say "Sam asked to use Acme's recovery kit on 3 November."
- **The wait is 48 hours,** the figure agreed in October.
- **Only an Owner's approval releases sooner,** and never the requester's own. The second draft let any data admin approve, so one Admin and one kit holder could have taken a company whose Owner was on holiday.
- **Any Admin can object** (route C11). An objection makes the wait 7 days from the request and mails every Owner again.
- **Any Owner can cancel** until the release.
- With every Owner lost, the wait is what completes it: 48 hours, or 7 days where someone objected.
- After the release the server serves the requester the kit seal and the escrow. Their device opens the company seed with the kit and the group seed with the escrow, and signs the recovery roster (2.1, rule 11), with the kit's signature, on route C29.
- **After a recovery, earlier Owners are protected for 7 days.** For 7 days after a recovery roster the server holds every roster that changes the entry of anyone who was an Owner before it (removing, demoting or re-keying them), every suspension of such a person (C26), and every new kit, **whoever signs it**, unless its signer was already an Owner on the roster before the recovery roster (409 `recovery-hold`). The third draft held only what the recovered person signed. They could have made an accomplice an Owner in one roster, which needs no kit, and the accomplice, who was not held, could have removed the earlier Owner in the next. An earlier Owner who comes back in that week removes the recovered one at once. With no earlier Owner coming back, nothing is lost by the wait: from the first minute the recovered Owner lets people in, removes Members and Admins and changes folder keys. A **server rule**.
- **Released is exposed.** From the release the requester holds both seeds whether or not they ever post the roster. The server records the release in the ledger and tells every member's devices. If no recovery roster lands within 7 days, every Owner and Admin is told to treat the company key as exposed and replace it.
- **What these waits stand on.** The 48 hours, the approval, the objection, the cancel and the 7-day hold are **server rules**, every one. They hold against a kit holder at an honest server. They do not hold against the server. And they do not apply at all to someone who has the kit and a copy of the database: that person reads every company file without asking anyone (9.3). The chain's part is narrower and firmer: without the kit nobody signs a recovery roster, and with it nobody does so unseen.

**Two-person rules.** As agreed: with two or more data admins, adding a data admin and replacing the company key or kit by choice need a second data admin's approval; with one, a 48-hour delay and an email to every Owner. Using the kit has its own, stricter rule, above.

- They are **server rules**. The first admin's device files the change (`group_pending_changes`); a second data admin approves it from their own session, or the wait runs out; only then does the server accept the roster call that carries it.
- A removal is never held: taking away `data_admin`, or removing an admin, lands at once, with the forced key replacement. What stops one Admin removing everyone is rules 8 and 12, which are the chain's and not the server's: an Admin cannot remove an Owner or another Admin at all. The removals the server does hold are of an earlier Owner, for 7 days after a recovery (above).
- An Admin may make another Admin under this rule, without an Owner: it gives roles and makes no key (2.1, rule 12).
- No hold applies while the company has one active member: there is nobody else's work to protect, and a founder adding their first Admin should not wait two days (decision 10.25).
- A hostile server can skip the rule. What it cannot do is hide the result: every device sees the new admin on the roster, and members' apps say "Dana became an admin, added by Maya" from the roster itself.

### 2.3 Area lists: who opens what

A company roster names the company's people. It does not say who opens which folder. Each area has its own signed list:

```
["hushos/group/area-list",1,groupId,workspaceId,listVersion,previousListDigest,workspaceKeyVersion,signerUserId,signerKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,nonce,everyone,people]
people   = [[userId,role], ...]        sorted by userId, no duplicates, at most 10,000
role     = "manager" | "editor" | "viewer"
everyone = "" | "editor" | "viewer"
nonce    = 16 random bytes, new in every list statement
```

- It carries the signer's hybrid identity signature. It needs no group signature, so a folder's manager can sign one without holding the group key.
- `listVersion` counts up through the life of the area, across key versions. The first base list is list 1. The base list at key version `n` is numbered at least `n`, and a device refuses one that is not: every key change has a base list, so a lower number is one that some earlier list already had. That is the bound every device can check from the chain alone, a device that has only just been let in included. The signer's device does better than the bound (`signBaseAreaList`, Rust `area_list_base_sign`): it numbers the list one past the furthest of what it has verified and what any roster committed, and refuses to sign while it is behind or forked, so a new base list never takes a number an addition already has. A base list numbered at or below a head an earlier roster committed is a fork on every device that holds that head (below).
- `everyone` gives every active member of the company that role. An employee area's list is exactly its assignee, as manager, with `everyone` empty.
- `previousListDigest` is `""` for a base list, otherwise SHA-256 of the list before it.
- The `nonce` is there because list digests are on the roster and in the heads rows, which every member sees. Without it a colleague could find a small folder's people by hashing guesses.

**Base lists and additions.**

- A **base list** is the list at a folder key version. Its digest is on the roster row that introduced that version (`listDigest`, 2.1), so it is covered by the chain and by every mark. Its signer is that roster's signer. Its `rosterVersion` and `rosterDigest` name the roster **before** the one whose row commits it (it cannot name a roster that contains its own digest). For an area on roster 1 they are `0` and `""`: the list statement's grammar allows that pair, together and nowhere else, and a device accepts it only for a base list whose digest roster 1's rows carry. Everyone it names must be an active entry on the roster that commits it.
- An **addition** is a later list at the same key version. It may only add people, raise a role, or raise `everyone`. Its signer is active on the roster it names, which is on the reader's chain, and is a manager on the list before it, or holds `members`. Everyone it adds is an active entry on the roster it names: a list never names someone who is not yet, or no longer, a member. An addition that would change nothing is not signed (`signAreaListAddition`, Rust `area_list_addition_sign`): a list that adds nobody, raises no role, and gives nobody back an entry from before they left would only be a version for a server to play against a real one. The refusal says which, so an app can say "Sam is already on Clients".
- Anything that takes access away (removing a person, lowering a role, lowering `everyone`, reassigning an employee area) is a new base list, and so a key change on a roster. The server stops serving the person at once; the key change makes it true for keys.

**Why this shape.** A removal from a folder must be final and visible to every device, and must not depend on what the server chooses to serve: so it is on the roster. An addition happens often and takes nothing away: so it does not need an Admin or a roster.

**Heads: what every roster's signer saw.** An addition is off the roster, so a server could show it to the person added and to nobody else. Later that person leaves, and on every other device the folder they were quietly on is not owed a key change. So every roster commits to all the lists (and share logs, 2.4) its signer's device had verified:

```
headsDigest = SHA-256(["hushos/group/heads",1,groupId,rows])
rows        = [[workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,listVersion,listDigest,shareLogIndex,shareLogDigest], ...]     every live area, sorted by workspaceId
```

- The roster call uploads the rows that differ from the roster before (`group_roster_heads`). The server rebuilds the full set and refuses a call whose rows do not hash to `headsDigest` (`invalid` (`heads`)). It serves each roster's rows with it on route 5. All of it is public to the server already.
- **A row must exist.** The server refuses a row that names a list version, or a share log position, that it has not stored with that digest (`invalid` (`heads-unknown`)). Otherwise a buggy or hostile signer could commit a head that does not exist, and every device would stop writing in that folder. This is a **server rule**; where the server does not apply it, the way out is that folder's key change ("A committed head nothing reaches", below).
- **A removal must be current.** The server refuses a roster that marks someone `left` when what it has stored for the areas that person was entitled in holds **an entry that person signed** (a list addition, or a share log record of theirs) that the roster's rows do not cover (409 `heads-behind`, naming the areas). Other people's activity in those folders does not refuse a removal; the next roster's heads take it in. The third draft compared whole heads, so a busy folder could have kept refusing the removal of someone who had done nothing in it. The remover's device reads, verifies and signs again. A departure holds those areas' row locks (3.11), so nothing lands between the check and the write. A **server rule**, like the lock itself. Without this, an honest addition the leaver made a moment before their removal would fall outside the removal's heads and be refused for good, with every later list built on it.
- **A roster must not be behind a restriction.** The server refuses a roster whose row for an area stops short of a stop, a batch stop or a record that lowered a role or brought an expiry forward, which it has stored in that area's share log (409 `heads-behind`, `data.reason: 'restriction'`, naming the areas). The signer's device reads, verifies and signs again. For an area the roster's own rows touch, a key change above all, the check is made under that area's row lock, which a share log append takes too, so it is exact: at an honest server a key change is never signed over a log that lacks a stop the server holds. For the other areas it is made without their locks (3.11), so a stop stored in the same instant can slip past; that leaves devices ahead of a roster that changed no key there, which is routine (below). The recovery roster is exempt: rule 11 gives it the heads of the roster before, whatever has been stored since. A **server rule**.
- **A committed head never moves back.** The server refuses a roster whose row gives an area a list version or a share log position below one an earlier roster committed for it (409 `heads-behind`, `data.reason: 'committed'`). And a device does not depend on that: **the committed head of an area is the furthest one any roster on the device's chain committed for it**, the highest list version and the highest log position. A later roster with a lower row for that area moves nothing; the device checks that the lower point lies on the same record (a fork otherwise) and reports the roster (route C33, `ahead`). Without this, an Admin's device working from a stale read, or a hostile Admin, could sign heads that are stored but old, and put every device that had seen more "ahead".
- **A roster only for heads.** When a device has reported an override or settled a fork in an area (`due.heads`, with `why: 'override'` or `'fork'`), an Admin's device signs a roster at its next sync that changes nothing but `heads`: `members` as `["same", …]`, no area row, the same `custody` and `rules`, an empty `auto`. It commits what that Admin's device has verified, the re-posted entries included, and is what lets an override go (2.4). It draws on the owed budget (3.11), and a device signs one only for those two reasons: an area that is merely ahead waits for the next ordinary roster.
- **The core builds the rows** (`headsToCommit`, Rust `heads_to_commit`), and `nextRoster` / `company_roster_next` take their heads from nothing else. The builder is given the lists the device read at its latest roster, one for every live folder; it refuses while any of them is behind or forked (a folder whose key this roster changes is exempt, since its new base list could only be signed past everything read), and it never writes a row below the furthest an earlier roster committed, for the list or for the share log. So an honest device cannot sign a roster from a stale read, and cannot lower a head. The heads it returns fit one roster: the one after the roster they were read at, with the area rows they were built with.
- A signer's device commits the heads **it verified**, never ones the server merely reported. An Admin's device reads every folder's list and share log to do so, in bulk (route C34), verifying only what is new since its own record. That reading is not browsing and is not in the ledger. It shows an Admin who is on every folder, and what is shared from each, by id, and the disclosure says so (3.7, decision 10.36).

**What a device keeps.** For each area it has opened: the furthest committed head it verified (the roster's version, the list's version and digest, the share log's index and digest), and every list and log entry it has verified past that head, its own included; and its overrides (2.4).

**Reading a new committed head.** When a roster on the device's chain commits a head for an area that is further than the one the device holds, the device fetches what lies between the two and checks that it chains from the one to the other. (A roster whose row is not further moves nothing, as above.) Then exactly one of four things is true, **whatever the folder's key version.** (The second draft compared only "at the same key version". That excused the roster that changes a folder's key, which is the one roster where a hidden stop does harm.)

| The device is | Meaning                                                                                                                 | What it does                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Level**     | The committed head is where the device's record ends                                                                    | Nothing                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Behind**    | The committed head is past the device's record, and what the server serves extends the record to it                     | Verifies the new entries and goes on. Until it has, it does not write in that area (`HEAD_BEHIND`)                                                              |
| **Ahead**     | The committed head is a point on the device's own verified record, before its end. The signer had seen less             | Keeps its record, posts the entries past the head again and reports (route C33, `ahead`). Routine after a race. It tells the person only in the two cases below |
| **Forked**    | Anything else: the committed head is not on the device's record, and nothing the server serves extends the record to it | Warns at once, posts every entry it holds that the committed record lacks (C33, `fork`), and stops writing in that area until the fork is settled (below)       |

- **Ahead, and nothing more.** A device ahead of a roster that changed no key in that area has lost nothing: what the signer had not seen is still in the device's own record, every other device reads it from the server as it syncs, and the next roster's heads take it in. A stop that is ahead in this way is an ordinary stop (2.4, "Writes wait"); it makes no override.
- **Ahead, and it matters.** If the next roster still does not include what the device posted again, the person sees a calm notice: "Acme's record is missing a change to who can open Clients. An admin needs to look." The person who made an addition is always such a device, so a hidden addition is noticed by its maker. And if the roster is one that changed that area's key, what the signer missed may already have had its effect (the next point, and 2.4 for a missed stop).
- **A new base list replaces what was before it.** A key change's base list is signed from the list as the rotator's device verified it. An addition it never saw is not in it, so the person added loses the folder at that key change: a hidden addition ends as a removal, which is the safe direction. The addition's maker is ahead at that roster and is told: "Sam is no longer on Clients after a key change. Add them again." A device works that out the same way at every key change it reads with lists in hand, whether or not the new base list is numbered past them: everyone its lists had added beyond the head it held committed, whom the new list does not cover, is reported lost, and those lists are marked stale (`list-stale`). The first build reported them only when the new list's number collided with one the device held, so a base list numbered past a hidden addition dropped its person without a word.
- **Forked, and what the person sees.** Something this device verified is not in the record the company's signers committed to, and it is not merely further along. The device does not wait for entries that will never come. It says, at once: "Acme's record of who can open Clients doesn't match what this device saw. An admin needs to look." The server lists the area in `due.heads` for the Admins.
- **Settling a fork.** The server's record is the only one the company can go on with. Once the device has warned and posted what was missing, it takes the committed record as its base **for chaining**: later entries are checked as extending the committed head. What it posted is appended where it is still valid: a stop for a share that is still open lands as that share's next record. An addition built on a list version that is gone is refused (`list-stale`), and its maker is told to add the person again.
- **It does not forget what it verified.** Taking the committed record as a base drops nothing that took access away. Every restrictive entry the device verified and the committed record lacks (a stop, a record that lowered a role or brought an expiry forward) is kept as an **override** (2.4, "Overrides"), and the device goes on treating that share as stopped or lowered until the committed record says so too. The third draft's device simply adopted the server's record, and with it forgot the stop it had seen: its writes under that node resumed, and if it walked, it re-sealed the share. A list has no such entry off the roster: additions only add, and anything that takes access away is a base list on a roster. So a list fork can cost someone a folder, never give one.
- **The note, and who clears it.** A fork leaves a note on the device that saw it, on every device of that person, and for the Admins (`due.heads`).
    - **The person clears their own note, on their own device,** after reading it. Nobody else can.
    - **An Admin dismisses it for the Admins with a signed statement,** hybrid, under their identity:

        ```
        ["hushos/group/fork-dismissal",1,groupId,workspaceId,kind,committedPosition,committedDigest,reportedPosition,reportedDigest,signerUserId,signerKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest]
        kind = "list" | "share-log"
        ```

        It names both records: the head the roster committed and the head the device reported. The server verifies it under the Admin's roster entry before it stores it (route C33), serves it to the area's people, and a device hides the Admins' note only for a dismissal that verifies and names the two digests of the fork it saw. One signed under keys its signer has since replaced verifies for nobody ("After someone's keys are replaced", below). The third draft's dismissal was an unsigned call, which the server could have claimed on its own.

    - A dismissal clears a note. It clears no override: only the committed record does.
- **A committed head nothing reaches.** If a roster commits a head past the end of the device's record and the server, asked for it, answers with nothing that reaches it, the device is forked and says so. A request that fails is tried again and is not an answer.
    - **What lets a device go on.** Only this case has a way on without the head being reached: when a later roster's row for the folder is lower, and the device's record reaches that row, the device judges by it. It does not do so silently: it keeps a note naming the head it passed over and the roster that committed it, returns that note with the reading, and reports (route C33, `fork`). The note is the person's to clear, like any other.
    - **A true fork is never cleared this way.** A committed head at a version the device's record holds, with another digest, is a fork whatever any later roster says, and so is a head on the far side of lists that do not chain from the record. A later, lower row settles neither. Otherwise a hostile Admin could commit a forked head and then sign it away with a stale one. Those forks end as "Settling a fork" says.
    - **An honest signer's way out is the folder's key change.** No honest device signs the lower row: the builder refuses while a folder is forked and never lowers a head (above). What it can sign is that folder's key change. `signBaseAreaList` allows a new base list on a forked folder in this one case, where the committed head lies past everything the device holds, and numbers it past that head; the roster that commits it starts the folder's record again at the new key, and every device is level. Until an Admin's device does that, it signs no other roster either, since its heads would be built over a folder it cannot read. The earlier drafts said "the next honest roster" clears the warning; with a builder that refuses to lower a head, the next honest roster is this one.
- A device never sits behind a head for good, and never drops something it verified without saying so. Those two sentences are what the tests in section 7 hold.

**After someone leaves.** A signature carries no time, and a list names the roster its signer chose. So a removed manager could sign an addition today that names a roster from before they left. To stop that, an addition whose signer is no longer entitled to sign it on the reader's head roster (marked `left`, or `members` taken away where that was their standing) is accepted only if it is within the heads committed by the roster that ended their standing: its `listVersion` is at most that roster's `listVersion` for the area, at the same key version. Anything of theirs past it is refused (`AFTER_LEAVING`), and so is every list built on it. The same rule covers their share records (2.4). An honest addition they made before leaving is within those heads when the server is honest, because a removal that is behind is refused (above). One that a hostile server kept from the remover is refused; the person it added loses the folder at its next key change, and an Admin adds them again.

**After someone's keys are replaced.** Replacing keys is what a person does when a device is lost or stolen, and the same hole is there: whoever holds the old keys could sign an addition today that names a roster from before the change, and every device would check it under the keys that roster named. So a key change is treated like leaving. Where a later roster on the reader's chain moved the signer's entry to other signing keys, an addition under the earlier keys is accepted only if it is within the heads committed up to that roster: its `listVersion` is at most the furthest any roster up to and including that one committed for the area, at the same key version. Past it, it is refused (`KEYS_REPLACED`), with every list built on it, and its maker signs it again under their new keys. This holds for an Admin's old keys on a folder they manage only by role, as for a manager's. Where the signer also left, the earlier of the two rosters bounds them. A fork dismissal under keys since replaced is refused outright: there is no head for it to be within, and the Admin signs it again. An honest addition made a moment before a fold-in, which the fold-in's signer had not read, is refused in the same way as one made a moment before a removal; the server's "a removal must be current" rule (above) should cover a fold-in too, and that is PR 4's to add. **PR 3's share records need this bound as well as the leaving bound** (2.4): a share, stop or batch stop under keys since replaced counts only within the share log position committed up to the roster that replaced them.

**After someone comes back.** A person who left and is let in again starts with nothing. A list entry counts for them only when the list that carries it names a roster at or after the one that last let them in (the latest roster on the device's chain where their entry went from absent or `left` to `active`). So a base list from before their removal, on a folder whose key has not changed yet, does not hand back the standing they had.

**Verifying.** A device verifies an area's list from the base list the chain commits to, along the additions, each signed by someone entitled on the list before it. It keeps what "What a device keeps" says, in its device record (never synced). A served list that does not extend that record is a fork (above), never silently taken. Where the server serves two lists that both verify at one version (a manager's two devices racing, or one signer playing two), every device takes the same one, the one whose digest sorts lowest, refuses the other (`list-rival`) and reports a fork (C33) though it goes on: which list stands does not depend on the order the server chose to serve them in, and the people only the other list added are told to be added again.

**Who is entitled.** One resolver, on the device (`areaAccess`, Rust `area_access`) and on the server (`packages/db/src/membership.ts`, replacing "holds the workspace key and is an active member" for `org` areas):

| Basis                     | Who                                                                                         | What                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal owner            | The workspace is theirs                                                                     | Everything, as today                                |
| Folder list               | Active on the roster, not suspended, and on the folder's latest list (or `everyone` is set) | Their role there                                    |
| Share grantee             | Named in a live share whose record verifies (2.4), on that node's subtree                   | Viewer or editor                                    |
| Data admin, browsing      | Active with `data_admin`, in a browse session (3.7)                                         | Everything except making shares and links           |
| Data admin, changing keys | Active with `data_admin`, in a key-change session for an area that is owed one (3.6)        | The rotation work and nothing else: no download URL |

The rows are tried in that order, and the folder's list comes before a session: a data admin who is on a folder's list acts there by the list, at their role there, whether or not a browse or key-change session is open. They are not browsing a folder they are on, and a session does not widen or narrow what the list gives them (a viewer on the list who opens a browse session is still a viewer there, and the ledger records no browse). The first build let an open session win.

Membership of the company alone grants nothing. The server's denied-path suite (section 7) is built on that sentence.

**Grants.** The area grant statement is unchanged. For an `org` area a grantee's device opens a grant only if, beside `groups-design.md` 2.6's rules, the grantee is entitled by the list it verified. The same holds for sealing one (`sealAreaGrant` with the verified list, Rust `company_area_grant_seal`): a device seals a company folder's key only to someone its verified list entitles, and to nobody without a list; the family calls (`area_grant_seal`, `area_grant_open`) refuse a company's chain. A company grant's granter is judged at the roster the grant names, by the keys and the standing their entry had there (2.2, "Whose signature a seal is checked under"): a grant made by an Admin opens after that Admin is removed, as their folder seals do, and one that names a roster where they were not active opens for nobody. Grants owed change accordingly: a roster call that creates or rotates an area owes the new key's grant to everyone on its base list who is active and not suspended (the uploader included), and an admission owes the newcomer the grants of every folder whose list has `everyone` set. A suspended member is granted nothing while paused; when they are resumed, `due.grants` lists what they lack and any entitled device fills it (route 22).

**Who writes.** A reader judges a writer's signature in an `org` area by the roster and by the latest list it verified. `groups-design.md` 2.8's order holds: no signature first, then a roster that did not load, then an entry marked `left`, then a personal pin and key conflicts, as written there. **Then the folder's own rows, for every writer who is active on the roster and whose signature holds, whether it holds under the roster's keys or under a pin the reader made themselves.** A personal check says whose keys these are. It does not say the person is on this folder, and 2.8 already ranks leaving above a pin. In order:

| The writer                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Verdict              | Shown                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any, while the reader's list for this folder did not load or verify, or is behind or forked from a head a roster committed                                                                                                               | `unchecked`          | Couldn't check (fails closed)                                                            |
| An editor or manager on the list, or an active member where `everyone` is `editor`                                                                                                                                                       | `verified`, by group | No mark; Info: "Verified by Acme"                                                        |
| An active data admin not on the list                                                                                                                                                                                                     | `verified`, by group | No mark; Info: "Maya, an admin of Acme"                                                  |
| A colleague who edits that subtree by a share record that verifies                                                                                                                                                                       | `verified`, by group | No mark. The roster vouches for a colleague's keys anywhere in the company's areas (3.3) |
| Someone outside the company who edits that subtree by a share record that verifies                                                                                                                                                       | as without groups    | As a contact reads: Not verified unless the reader checked them                          |
| Active on the roster, the signature holds under their roster keys or the reader's own pin, and none of the rows above: a viewer, someone on an earlier list, a former data admin, or an editor whose addition this device was not served | `not-on-this-folder` | Drawn as Unverified: "Written by Sam, who isn't on this folder now"                      |

- **`not-on-this-folder` is never a bad signature.** The signature held. What the reader cannot confirm is the writer's standing on the folder, and a server can cause that on its own by keeping an addition back. Round 3's rule (2.8) stands here: anything a server alone can cause reads "couldn't check" or Unverified, and `bad-signature` is kept for a signature that fails under the keys the roster or the reader's own pin names.
- The second draft sent every one of the last row's cases to "a bad signature", which would have read an honest editor as a forger whenever the server withheld one list. It also read a colleague who edits by a share as "Not verified unless pinned", against 3.3.
- The third draft put these rows in place of 2.8's "active on the roster, no pin" row only. A reader who had checked Sam then read `verified` from the pin row, with Sam off the folder.
- Former members, key conflicts, re-keyed records and retired keys read as 2.8 has them, unchanged.

Roster trust stays scoped to the company's areas, with first-seen pins outside (2.8, unchanged).

**Residuals.**

- A signature carries no trustworthy time. A record written by someone while they were an editor reads as "no longer on this folder" after they are taken off, as a former member's does.
- Between someone's removal from a list and the folder's key change, they still hold the key. An honest server serves them nothing. Uploads into that folder wait meanwhile (3.6).
- A manager with a modified app can add anyone in the company to a folder. That is what a manager is.
- Heads give detection, not prevention. A server that hides an addition from every signer, or stores another entry in its place, is caught by the addition's maker at the next roster, and not before.
- Every member sees, in the roster rows and heads, every folder's id, when it was made or re-keyed, and how often its list and share log change. Names, people and contents are not there. Hiding the rest would take per-folder commitments off the roster, which is not designed.

### 2.4 Share and link records

This finishes `groups-design.md` 3.4's proposal and replaces its version 1 layout; PR 6 updates that section to point here. It applies to every group area, family and company.

**Why.** A key change re-seals a node's shares and links under its new key. In a personal space the walker knows which are genuine from the owner's sealed sharing ledger, which the owner's device clears before a stop reaches the server. In a group folder each member has their own ledger, so a walker can vouch only for their own shares; re-sealing what the server lists would let the server add recipients. That is why sharing in family folders is refused today (409 `family-sharing`). Signed records fix adding. They do not by themselves fix stopping: a server that kept a stopped share's record could put the row back before a walk. The share log is for that.

**The share record.** The granter signs it, hybrid, when they share or change a share:

```
["hushos/group/share",2,groupId,workspaceId,nodeId,shareId,seq,granteeUserId,granteeKeyVersion,granteeKeyDigest,role,granterUserId,granterKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,expiresAt]
granteeKeyDigest = SHA-256(encryptionPublicKey (32) ‖ signingPublicKey (32) ‖ mlDsaPublicKeyDigest (32) ‖ kemPublicKeyDigest (32))
expiresAt        = "" | a 24-character UTC time, as a pin's `checkedAt` (`groups-design.md` 2.7)
```

**The link record.**

```
["hushos/group/link",2,groupId,workspaceId,nodeId,linkId,seq,tokenDigest,passwordSet,granterUserId,granterKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,expiresAt]
tokenDigest = the link's stored token hash (SHA-256 of its token)
passwordSet = 0 | 1
```

**The stop record.** Whoever stops a share or link signs it, hybrid:

```
["hushos/group/share-stop",1,groupId,workspaceId,nodeId,kind,subjectId,seq,nodeKeyEpoch,signerUserId,signerKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest]
kind         = "share" | "link"
nodeKeyEpoch = the shared node's key epoch when it was stopped
```

**The batch stop.** After a removal an Admin answers for everything the leaver shared (3.6). One signed entry stops all of it in a folder:

```
["hushos/group/share-stop-all",1,groupId,workspaceId,granterUserId,throughLogIndex,throughLogDigest,except,signerUserId,signerKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest]
except = [subjectId, ...]        sorted, no duplicates, at most 1,000: the shares and links being kept
```

- It stops every share and link in that folder whose latest entry **before the batch stop's own place in the log** is a record that names `granterUserId` as its granter and sits at or before `throughLogIndex`, except those listed. For each of them it is the last entry: nothing follows it, as nothing follows a stop.
- **A handed-on record wins.** A share an Admin kept has, as its latest entry, a record that names its new granter. A batch stop appended after that does not cover it, whatever position the batch stop was signed over. So a second Admin's batch stop, built before the first Admin's answers and sent after them, closes nothing the first one kept. (Judged by `throughLogIndex` alone it would have.)
- It is accepted only when `granterUserId` is marked `left` on the roster it names, its signer holds `members` there (in a family too), and `throughLogIndex` and `throughLogDigest` are a position on the log as the reader has verified it. It carries the signer's hybrid identity signature.
- It names no key epoch. Every folder a leaver could share from is one they were entitled in, so the removal already made that folder's whole key change owed (3.6), and writes there wait for that.
- A kept share still needs its own record, signed by its new granter ("Handing on", below).

- `seq` is 1 for a share's or link's first record and one more for each record after it. A stop is the last: its `seq` is the latest record's plus one, and nothing follows it. A share id or link id is never used again.
- **Who may share.** In a family, any active member. In a company, someone entitled as editor or manager by the folder's list at the roster the record names, or the assignee of an employee area, within the company's policy (3.5). A share grantee does not share on, as today (`shareNode` requires a workspace member).
- **Who may stop.** In a family, the granter or a `members` holder (decision 10.32). In a company, the granter, a manager of the folder, or a `members` holder.
- **Changing** a share's role or expiry, or a link's password or expiry, is a new record with the next `seq`, signed by its granter.
- **Handing on** (3.6): an Admin or the folder's manager signs the next record as its new granter. It is accepted with another `granterUserId` than the record before only when the earlier granter is marked `left` on the roster it names. The grantee and the secret do not change.

**The share log.** Each group area has one, append-only, in the order the server stored the entries:

```
logDigest(0) = SHA-256("hushos/group/share-log/v1" ‖ groupId (36 ASCII bytes) ‖ workspaceId (36))
logDigest(i) = SHA-256(logDigest(i−1) ‖ SHA-256(statement(i)))
```

- An entry is a share, link or stop record. The server appends it under the area's row lock. Making, re-sealing and stopping a share or link take that lock today; changing a link without a new seal does not (`updateLink`, `packages/db/src/drive.ts`), and gains it in a group area. It checks before storing: its shape, its binding to the row it sits on, its signature under the signer's roster entry keys (as it does for seals), that its `seq` is the stored one plus one, that the subject is not stopped, and that the signer may sign it.
- The log is kept for the life of the area, stops included. The server may drop the signatures of entries that are both superseded and at or before the latest head a roster committed; statements stay.
- **Budgets.** Every signer verifies every new entry before it signs a roster, so without a ceiling one member's app could make each roster cost minutes. Three **server rules** bound it, each an operator setting:
    - a share or link takes at most 20 records in its life (409 `share-budget`: stop it and make another);
    - a member has at most 500 live shares and links of their own making in one company or family (409 `share-limit`);
    - a member appends at most 1,000 entries a day there (429 `rate-limited`), a backstop against an app that makes and stops shares in a loop, which the live cap alone does not bound.
- **What never counts against anyone's budget:** a stop signed by a `members` holder, a walker's stop for a share it drops, a batch stop, and a hand-over record. The third draft held all of those to 100 a day. An Admin offboarding someone who had made a few hundred shares would have needed days to answer for them, with the folders read-only meanwhile.
- Everyone entitled to the folder by its list reads the whole log, so everyone who can open a folder sees who it is shared with. That answers 3.4's open question, and it is the claim the product already makes ("Every file and folder says who can open it"). A share grantee is served the records of the node shared with them and nothing else.

**Where the log's head is committed.**

- **A company:** in every roster's `heads` (2.3), the rotation's own roster included.
- **A family** (format 2 has no `heads`): the call that rotates an area carries a checkpoint signed, hybrid, by that roster's signer:

    ```
    ["hushos/group/share-log-head",1,groupId,workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,logIndex,logDigest,signerUserId,signerKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest]
    ```

    A device requires one for every area key version whose log is not empty, and treats a missing one as a head it is behind. The server refuses a family roster call that rotates an area whose log is not empty without the checkpoint for that area (`invalid` (`share-log-head`)), on every route that carries rotations (7, 18, 19 and 24), and refuses one that names a position it has not stored, a position before an earlier checkpoint's, or one that stops short of a stop, batch stop or lowering record it has stored for that area (409 `heads-behind`, as for a company's roster, 2.3; exact, since a rotation holds the area's row lock). Those refusals are **server rules**; the device's own rule is the sentence before. If a rotation lands without one all the same, any `members` holder's device posts it afterwards (route C35), signed over the log as that device had verified it at the rotation's roster.

**What a device does with the log.** What it does with lists (2.3, "Reading a new committed head"): it is level, behind, ahead or forked, whatever the key version, against the furthest head a roster on its chain (or a family checkpoint) has committed.

- It verifies entries as it reads them, and keeps the furthest committed head it verified and every entry past it. A served log that does not extend what it holds is a fork, reported and warned, never silently taken.
- A new device starts from the latest head its chain (or a family checkpoint) commits: it hashes the entries up to that head, which proves it has the log that signer verified, and verifies signatures only for the latest record of each live share or link and for every entry after the head.
- **Behind:** it catches up before it writes or walks there.
- **Ahead, at a roster that changed no key there:** nothing to do but post again (2.3). The stop is in this device's log and in the log every other device reads; the ordinary wait applies ("Writes wait", below).
- **Ahead, at a roster that changed the area's key** (or a family checkpoint): the walk was made by a device that had seen less of the log than this one. At an honest server this does not happen: the server refuses a key change signed over a log that lacks a stop it holds (2.3). If what the signer missed includes a stop, or a record that lowered a role or shortened a time, the walk may have re-sealed a share that had been stopped or lowered. The device keeps each such entry as an override (below), tells the server (route C33), which makes that node's subtree owed again, and tells the folder's managers: "A share that was stopped may still be open: Clients / Budget.xlsx. An admin or a manager needs to change its keys." Any device that saw the stop does this, the stopper's included.
- **Forked:** the same warning and the same report, at once. This is the case the second draft missed. A server that hides a stop from the walker and then stores someone else's entry at that position leaves the stopper's device with a record the committed one does not contain. That device used to read itself as behind, and waited for entries that would never come. Now it warns, posts the stop again (it lands as the next record of a share that is still open, and the subtree is owed again), and takes the committed log as its base for chaining, keeping the stop as an override (below).

**Overrides.** What a device keeps of a restrictive entry it verified, when the committed log does not have it.

- **What makes one.** Two things only:
    - a fork (2.3, "Forked");
    - a roster that changed that area's key, or a family checkpoint, whose head for the area leaves the device ahead.

    In either, each entry the device verified and the committed log lacks that is a stop, a batch stop, or a record that lowered a role or brought an expiry forward becomes an override. **Being ahead of a roster that changed no key there makes none.** The fourth draft made one whenever a device was ahead. On an honest server a stop stored a moment before a roster whose signer had not read it yet would then have left every device that saw the stop holding an override, refusing writes under that item until some later roster happened to commit it, while devices that synced a minute later behaved normally. A stop that a key change did not pass over is still just a stop: it is in the log everyone reads.

- **What it holds.** The area and the entry itself with its signature, and what the entry restricts: for a stop or a lowering record, the node, the share or link id, the entry's `seq` and what it restricts to (stopped, or the role and expiry it set); for a batch stop, the granter and the position it was signed over. It is in the device record (the `hushos-groups` store, sealed under the device's own key, never synced), with the lists and logs.
- **What the device does while it holds one.** It treats that share or link as the override says, whatever the server serves:
    - walking, it never re-seals it past the override: a stopped one is dropped, with a stop record of the walker's own on the committed log; a lowered one is re-sealed at the lowered role at most;
    - it does not write under that node (as under any stopped share's node, below);
    - it shows the share as "Stopped, not yet confirmed", and posts the entry again at each sync (route C33).
- **When it goes.** In one of two ways:
    - **a committed head covers it:** a head a roster (or a family checkpoint) commits holds an entry for that share or link that is at least as restrictive (a stop, or a record with that role or lower and that expiry or sooner); for a batch stop, a batch stop for that granter over that position or a later one;
    - **there is nothing left to restrict:** the node was deleted for good (the feed says it was purged; a node in Trash keeps its override, since Trash restores), or the folder was retired on the chain (its tombstone row, `groups-design.md` 2.10).

    Not when the node's key changes: a key change made by a device that never saw the stop re-seals the share under the new key, and the override is then the only thing on this device that still knows. Writes under the node need both: the override gone, and the node's key epoch past the stop's.

- **The way out, and how long it takes.** The device posts the entry again at once (C33). An honest server stores it, as the next record of a share the walk left open, makes the subtree owed again, and lists the area in `due.heads` with `why: 'override'`. An Admin's device signs a roster for heads at its next sync (2.3, "A roster only for heads"; in a family, the next rotation's checkpoint, which the stop has just made due). That head covers the stop, and the override goes on every device. So at an honest server an override lasts until an Admin's device is next online, not until some later roster happens along.
- **If the server never takes the entry back,** the override stays, that node stays read-only on this device, and the note stays. That is the honest state: this device knows of a stop the company's record does not have. An Admin ends it by stopping the share themselves, which puts a stop in the committed log. What a server gains by refusing is that one item's subtree staying read-only on the devices that saw the stop, which is less than its standing power to refuse writes.
- **Who can make one on someone else's device.** Nobody, by signing things. An override comes only from an entry the device itself verified, signed by someone entitled to stop that share, and only when a key change or a fork passed over it. A member cannot manufacture one for a colleague; a server can, by hiding a real stop from a walker, which is the attack overrides exist for.

**The person's other devices: settings marks.** An override is local, because an entry is 3.5 KB and settings are 64 KiB for everything. What is synced is a mark in the person's sealed settings document, in a new top-level field:

```
shareMarks = [mark, ...]                 at most 32, oldest first
mark = ["share",groupId,workspaceId,subjectKind,subjectId,seq,restricts,sha256(statement)]
     | ["granter",groupId,workspaceId,granterUserId,throughLogIndex,sha256(statement)]
     | ["area",groupId,workspaceId]
     | ["group",groupId]
subjectKind = "share" | "link"
restricts   = "stopped" | [role,expiresAt]
```

- A `share` mark is for an override of a stop or a lowering record. A `granter` mark is for an override of a batch stop: it has no share id and no `seq`, so it names the granter and the position the batch stop was signed over. Each is about 200 bytes; 32 of them are under 7 KiB.
- **What another device of the same person does with one.** For a `share` mark it treats that share or link as `restricts` says, in the three ways above (it does not re-seal past it, does not write under that node, and shows it as not yet confirmed). For a `granter` mark it treats every share and link in that folder whose latest record it holds names that granter as stopped. It takes a mark on its own person's word, which is all a restriction needs. It cannot post the entry, which it does not hold; if its person may stop that share, it offers to stop it again.
- **Who drops a mark.** The device that holds the override, when the override goes. **And any device of the person, once its own committed head covers the mark** by the rule above, or the node or folder is gone. So a mark does not outlive the lost or wiped device that wrote it: the fourth draft let only that device remove it, and its person's other devices would have treated the share as stopped for good.
- **The 33rd mark.** Marks are never dropped oldest-first: that would make the person's other devices forget a stop. When a 33rd is needed, the device folds every mark of the folder that has the most into one `area` mark; when no folder has more than one mark, it folds every mark of the company that has the most into one `group` mark. A device that reads an `area` mark knows only that another of its person's devices holds overrides somewhere in that folder. It does not walk that folder (it leaves its key changes to the device that holds the overrides, or to someone else's), re-seals nothing in it, and does not write under any item there that has a live share or link. A `group` mark is the same for every folder of the company. They are blunt, and they are what a person's second device does when a server has hidden more than 32 stops from walkers.
- **A coarse mark has no entry to compare with,** so only the device that wrote it lifts it on its own. On any other device the person can lift it by hand, after reading what it means ("Another of your devices found stops that Acme's record is missing. If that device is gone, clear this."). The same button lifts a `share` or `granter` mark.
- **Room in the document.** When the document is full, room for a mark is made by forgetting removals (`removedContacts`), oldest first, as room for a pin is made (`settingsThatFit`, `packages/crypto/src/pins.ts`): **a mark outranks a removal.** A pin is refused (`CONTACTS_FULL`) when writing it would leave less than 8 KiB of the document free (`SHARE_MARKS_RESERVE`), so marks have room of their own from the first release that writes them. An account whose document is already past that from before, with every removal forgotten, may have no room: the mark is then not written, the override still holds on the device that made it, and its note says "Your other devices could not be told."
- **Older clients.** A client from before this release keeps the field and ignores it. Settings stay document version 2, and clients keep top-level fields they do not know: the web seals the document it read with its own changes spread over it (`sealSettings`, `packages/crypto/src/contacts.ts`); the Android suite asserts that unknown fields survive a save (`SettingsKeyTest`); `optional-verification.md` 7.5 says the same of every shipped client. For iOS that is taken from that document and from `Settings.swift`'s comments, not from a test read for this one; PR 3 adds one for each client. Ignoring a mark costs nothing: such a client is below the floor PR 6 sets, so it can neither walk nor share in a group folder.
- **A server that keeps the newer settings back** leaves the second device as it was before: a device that never saw the stop. It cannot roll settings back on a device that has seen the newer copy.

**A walker re-seals a share only when:**

- the log's latest entry for it, in the log as the walker's device has verified it (at least up to the head its roster or checkpoint commits), is a record and not a stop;
- that record verifies under the granter's keys at the roster it names, which is on the walker's chain;
- the granter is active, and still entitled to share there, on the member list and folder list the walk is judged by (the staged ones, for a removal's key change); or the record is within the heads the granter's removal committed and an Admin has handed it on (3.6);
- the grantee is not marked `left` on a roster later than the one the record names. Someone who has left may be shared with again as an outsider, by a record that names a roster on which they are already `left`;
- the grantee's served identity is the one the record's digest names, or follows from it by signed key replacements, as a contact's does;
- the walker's device holds no override or settings mark for it ("Overrides", above). With one, it re-seals at most what the override allows; under an `area` or `group` mark it does not walk that folder at all;
- it has not expired, and the policy still allows it (3.5). **A walker takes the server's policy row only as a reason to drop** a share whose capped time has passed, never as a reason to keep one. What keeps a share is its record and its granter's standing, which the server cannot make. A server that lies about the policy closes shares, which is a denial; it cannot open one.

Otherwise the walker drops it: it signs a stop record for it, and the server revokes it as the walk passes.

- **A link** is re-sealed under the same test, and only when the secret the walker opens from the link's sealed secret hashes to the record's `tokenDigest`.

**After a stop: the subtree's keys.** Stopping does not take back the key the recipient has. Only a key change does.

- **In a company folder** the stop makes a key change due for that node's subtree, not for the whole area (`area_owed_subtrees`, with the stop's `nodeKeyEpoch`). The server lists it in `due.subtrees` for the folder's managers and for Admins, whose devices walk it. A manager who is not a `members` holder may start a Drive rotation in a company area only for a node the server lists as owed, so nobody starts one to stall a removal (the family rule, `groups-server.md` section 8, stays otherwise). A share on an area's top folder cannot be re-keyed by a subtree walk (design 2.10): stopping one makes the area's own key change owed, on a roster.
- **Writes wait.** A device that has the stop in its log, or holds an override or a mark for a share on that node, does not write under that node until the node's key epoch is above the stop's `nodeKeyEpoch` and no override is left. The server refuses such writes too (409 `rotating`). The same wait applies under a node whose live record names a grantee the device's chain marks `left`.
- An Admin about to change a whole folder's keys finishes any subtree walk running there first, as a removal does in a family (`rotating`).
- **In a family folder** a stop still marks the whole area, as built, because only a family's managers rotate.

**What the log gives, and does not.**

- A server cannot add a share: there is no record for it.
- **A device that saw a stop never acts against it.** Not when the server serves a shorter log, not when it stores another entry in the stop's place, and not after the fork is settled: the device keeps the stop, in its own log or as an override, until the company's record has one. It does not re-seal that share and does not write under that node. The same holds for the person's other devices that have read the mark, and for a lowered role or a shortened time.
- A server **can** hide a stop from a device that never saw it, and have that device walk. The share is then re-sealed and the grantee keeps reading. Every device that saw the stop notices at the next committed head and says so, whether the server shortened the log or put another entry where the stop was, and keeps saying so until the stop is in the record. That is detection after the fact. The alternative that prevents it, letting only a granter's own device re-seal their shares, would leave every client link in a folder closed after any key change until its granter next opens the app, which breaks the thing agencies use sharing for (decision 10.22).
- Outside editors hold no chain and no log. They rely on the server's refusals; a hostile server can let them write under a key that is owed a change.

**The server** refuses a share or link in a group area without a record once every shipped client sends them (the client floor of section 8, PR 6). It cannot check that an envelope opens.

**Residual.** A granter's device can sign a record for a grantee whose keys the server planted, as with any first-seen contact. Checking the contact is the remedy, before or after.

### 2.5 Activity records and the sealed ledger

Two things are written for a recorded request (3.8): the member's own record of it, which only the company's admins can read, and the server's fact of it, which the server seals to the company's key as it writes and cannot read back.

**The device's activity key.** A device makes, per company and company key version, a 32-byte symmetric key and an Ed25519 signing key, and registers them once:

```
["hushos/group/audit-key",1,groupId,custodyKeyVersion,actorUserId,actorKeyVersion,auditKeyId,auditSigningPublicKey,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,sha256(sealed)]
```

- `sealed` is the 32-byte key, sealed with suite 4 to the company key. HKDF `info`: `hushos/group/audit-key/v1`.
- The statement carries the member's hybrid identity signature, so it says whose device this is, under keys the roster names.
- The server verifies the signature under the actor's roster entry and stores it, with the month and nothing finer, and no device id. It rotates at least every 30 days and whenever the company key is replaced.
- Only an active member can register one. People a file is shared with from outside the company, and visitors of a link, are on no roster, and their only source for the company's key would be the server. Their requests carry no record (below).

**A detail record.**

```
ad        = ["hushos/group/audit",1,groupId,auditKeyId,seq,action,workspaceId,nodeId,subject,factDigest]
sealed    = nonce (24) ‖ XChaCha20-Poly1305(pad(detail), key = activity key, nonce, ad)
signature = Ed25519(auditSigningKey, ad ‖ SHA-256(sealed))
```

- `seq` counts up from 1 for each activity key, one per record. The device draws it; **the server does not track it.** A stored counter per device would be a readable tally of what each person did, and a strict order would break transfers that run side by side. An admin's device reads the numbers when it reads the ledger (below). A request that failed uses a number up, so a gap alone says nothing.
- `action` is one of 3.8's actions. `subject` is the version id, grantee id or link id the action is about, or `""`. For a thumbnails call it is SHA-256 of the canonical array of the version ids asked for, sorted.
- `factDigest` is SHA-256 of `["hushos/group/audit-request",1,groupId,action,workspaceId,nodeId,subject]`. The server builds it from the request it is handling and refuses a record whose fields or digest differ, or whose signature fails under the registered key. So a record cannot ride on another action.
- `detail` is canonical JSON: the item's name and path as the device saw them, its size, who it was shared with or the link's settings, why the bytes were read (`open`, `save`, `offline`, `preview`, `copy-out`), the device's name and the device's clock. It is padded to a Padmé bucket so its length does not give the name away.

**Why not a hybrid signature on every record.** One is 3,373 bytes and far slower to make than an Ed25519 signature; a download of a thousand files would write megabytes of signatures. The activity key is certified once under the member's hybrid identity signature, and records are signed Ed25519, as file and folder records are today (`identity-pq-design.md` 7). A data admin holds the symmetric key but not the signing key, so they cannot write a record in a member's name.

**The server's fact, sealed.** For each recorded request the server builds:

```
fact = ["hushos/group/audit-fact",2,groupId,at,actorUserId,deviceId,action,workspaceId,nodeId,subjects,basis,sessionId,reported,record]
subjects = [subject, ...]          one, or up to 100 version ids for a thumbnails call
basis    = "member" | "share" | "link" | "admin-browse" | "key-change" | "server"
record   = "" | [auditKeyId,seq,sealed,signature]         the member's detail record, whole
```

and stores only:

```
row = epochId ‖ nonce (24) ‖ XChaCha20-Poly1305(pad(fact), key = epochKey, nonce, ad = ["hushos/group/ledger-fact",1,groupId,epochId])
```

- **The epoch key.** A server process makes a random 32-byte key for a company when it first has a fact to write, and seals it once to the company's public keys: an ephemeral X25519 key and an ML-KEM-768 encapsulation, HKDF-SHA-256 with `info` `hushos/group/ledger-epoch/v1`, XChaCha20-Poly1305 with `ad = ["hushos/group/ledger-epoch",1,groupId,epochId,custodyKeyVersion]`. 1,192 bytes, in `group_ledger_epochs`. No signature: the server has no identity, and everything under an epoch key is the server's word.
- The process uses the key for at most ten minutes or 10,000 rows, never across a month's end or a company key replacement, and then stops using it and lets go of it. It is never written anywhere unsealed. One hybrid seal per ten minutes, instead of one per row, is what makes sealing every thumbnail read affordable.
- **What "lets go" is worth.** Bun cannot wipe memory. The key, and the facts it sealed, stay in the process's heap until the collector reuses that space. So a dump of a running server's memory can hold more than ten minutes' worth, and this document does not claim otherwise (threat model 8.7).
- **Order of writes.** The process commits the epoch's row, in a statement of its own, before it seals the first fact under that key. A fact names its epoch by a foreign key. So no fact ever names an epoch that a rollback took away.
- **A fact is committed before the answer it records is sent:** in the request's own transaction where the request writes, and in a statement of its own before a download or thumbnail URL is returned. A restart loses no fact for anything the server answered.
- **The company key's version is checked on every write,** not only in the process that replaced it. The insert is conditional on `groups.custody_key_version` still being the epoch's. When it is not, the process starts an epoch under the new key and writes again: another process, or another Admin, may have replaced the key a moment ago.
- Two processes make two epochs for the same ten minutes. That is two keys, not a fork.
- The member's whole detail record is inside the sealed row, so a stored row names no device, no person and no item. The row's readable columns are the company, the month, its id and its epoch.
- **Bound to the record.** The fact carries the record itself. An admin's device checks that the record's `ad` names the same action, area, node and subject as the fact around it, and that its signature verifies under an activity key the actor certified.

**The chain.** Once a minute, for each company with rows not yet linked, the worker (`groups.ledger`) adds a link:

```
linkDigest(0) = SHA-256("hushos/group/ledger/v1" ‖ groupId (36 ASCII bytes))
linkDigest(n) = SHA-256(linkDigest(n−1) ‖ u64be(n) ‖ u64be(rowCount) ‖ SHA-256(SHA-256(row) for each row stamped n, in id order))
```

- **The worker stamps rows.** In one transaction it takes the company's advisory lock, reads the rows whose `link_n` is null, writes link n (the number after the last) and sets `link_n = n` on exactly those rows. A row belongs to the link that stamped it, whatever its id. The second draft linked by id range ("every row up to id X"). Rows take their ids when they are inserted and commit in another order, so a row that committed after the worker had passed its id fell under no link, or broke the link an admin's device recomputed, which read as tampering on an honest server.
- **One writer per link.** The advisory lock, and the primary key `(group_id, n)` behind it, mean two workers cannot both write link n.
- **What the worker may change.** Its database role (the application's, on a deployment with one role) has INSERT and SELECT on the ledger tables, and UPDATE on the one column `link_n`. A trigger refuses an update that changes any other column, or a `link_n` that is already set. Nobody has DELETE; a month goes as a whole partition, dropped by the migration role's job (3.8).
- It is over ciphertext, so the server builds it without reading anything, and an admin's device checks it without trusting the order the server claims.
- It is per company and made by the worker, not in the request. A digest in every row would need a lock on every download: the first draft had one company-wide, and a lock on the area's row would still queue a thousand-file download behind every write. A link a minute costs nothing at request time.
- An admin's device remembers the latest link it verified (its number and digest, in its device record) and says so if what the server serves later does not continue it.
- **What it does not cover.** Rows that no link covers yet and no admin's device has read. Inside the current minute the server can leave a row out or put rows in another order, and nothing shows it.
- Links are kept as long as any row they cover. When a month's partition is dropped at twelve months, the links before it go too, and the oldest link kept is where a new device starts ("The log before December 2025 has been removed").

**What an admin's device can conclude.**

| It sees                                                                                                                                                                 | It can say                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A fact whose record verifies under a key the actor certified, with matching fields, that no other fact carries, and whose device clock is within 10 minutes of the fact | **Corroborated:** that person's device signed a request for this action on this item. The time is the server's.                                                                                                                                     |
| The same record, byte for byte, in more than one fact                                                                                                                   | Counted **once**, with "sent 3 times". A retry does this, and so can a server replaying a record. It never reads as the person doing the thing three times.                                                                                         |
| Two different records under one activity key with one `seq`                                                                                                             | "Sam's app used a number twice." Only that device can sign either, so this one is the app's doing.                                                                                                                                                  |
| A record whose device clock is more than 10 minutes from the fact's time                                                                                                | What the device signed, with "the times don't match". Not corroborated for when.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| A fact whose time is more than 15 minutes before the middle time (the median) of the link before its own                                                                | "Recorded late." Rows are linked within about a minute of being written, so this one was written late or given an earlier time.                                                                                                                     |
| A fact whose time is more than 15 minutes after the earliest time in the link after its own, or after the reading device's own clock                                    | "Timed ahead." It is shown as that, and left out of the comparison that marks other rows late. The third draft compared with the newest time in a link, so one fact dated next year would have made every honest row after it read "recorded late". |
| A fact whose record is missing, unreadable, or does not match (outsiders, link visitors, a modified app)                                                                | **The server's word.** Nothing a device can check says it happened, or that it was that person.                                                                                                                                                     |
| A record the app sent at its next sync with no request behind it                                                                                                        | **The app's word** (kept offline, printed, and the like; 3.8).                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Numbers a device never used                                                                                                                                             | Nothing. A request that failed uses one up.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

- **One record counts once.** The server tracks no sequence, so nothing stops it wrapping one genuine record in any number of facts, at times of its choosing. The rules above are what make that harmless: a device counts a record by its key, its number and its bytes, calls identical repeats retries, and never blames a person for them.
- **Time along the chain.** A fact's time is the server's, and the server could give an earlier one. An admin's device compares it with the record's own clock and with its place in the chain, and shows a fact that fits neither as written late. A server can still move a time by a few minutes without it showing.
- A sealed fact is therefore not proof. The server writes it, and a hostile server can write a false one that frames someone, or leave one out. What it cannot do is forge the signed record of an honest device, so the first row of the table is the only one the page calls corroborated. The first draft said "the fact is certain"; it is not.

**What sealing does and does not hide.**

- **A copy of the database shows no ledger.** It shows sealed rows, their months, and which ten-minute key and which link each belongs to: how much each company did, in order, with no time finer than the month.
- **It shows no read history.** In a company's areas the server does not write when an object was last read or when a link was last opened (section 5).
- **It still shows what Drive's own tables show in every space,** which is not nothing: who last wrote which item id, and when. Section 5 lists every column, and decision 10.38 is the owner's on whether those move inside the sealed metadata.
- A point-in-time backup that archives the database's write-ahead log holds the commit time of every insert, a sealed row's included. `docs/self-hosting.md` says so, and says to keep such archives no longer than the restore objective needs.
- A running server sees each request as it serves it. It cannot hand over a download without knowing which account asked for which object. For up to ten minutes it also holds the key to what it just wrote, and its memory may hold more (above).
- A dishonest live server can keep its own notes. No design in which a server serves the files prevents that, and `hushos.com/security` says so in those words.

### 2.6 Personal spaces stay out

The company key reaches company areas and nothing else. Four things hold that, each tested (section 7):

- **By construction on the device.** A custody seal is made only for an area the device's accepted chain lists for that company, at the version it lists, and the key sealed must have the commitment the chain lists. A personal workspace is on no company's chain, so no call can seal its key to a company.
- **By construction on the server.** `area_custody_seals` names its area and group together, as a foreign key to `workspaces(id, group_id)`. A personal workspace has no group, so no row can name one.
- **No bridge in the graph.** Nothing in a company area wraps a key of a personal workspace. Moving between spaces is a copy that re-encrypts under the target's keys. A share from a personal space to a colleague is an ordinary share to that person's identity; the company key is not a recipient of it.
- **The identity is the person's.** A data admin holds no seal of any member's account root, identity or settings key. A member's roster entry carries public keys only.

So an admin with the company key, the kit and the server's help still opens nothing in anyone's personal space. What a company can learn about a personal space is what its ledger records in company areas: that a file was downloaded there (3.8), not where it went. Nothing about a personal space is written to any company's ledger, sealed or not.

## 3. Flows

### 3.1 Creating a company

The creator's device:

1. derives the group id;
2. makes the group key, the company key and the kit;
3. shows the kit and has the person save it and type four of its words back;
4. signs both base lists, each naming roster `0` (2.3);
5. signs roster version 1 (format 3) with the creator as Owner, the `custody` field, `rules` with auto-join off, an empty `auto`, two areas, "Company" (`shared`, `everyone` as editor) and the creator's employee area, and `heads` over those two lists and their empty share logs;
6. seals: the group seed to itself, the company seed to itself and to the kit with the kit seal's proof, the group key's escrow, each area key to the company key, and its own grants; and derives each area's notice keys (3.7);
7. accepts that roster as a staged chain with the `created` anchor (`groups-design.md` 2.4), runs the own-entry check (3.1 there), and calls `POST /api/groups`.

The call's outbox entry, with the seeds in it, is kept until the first committed acceptance has written the device's cache and pin, exactly as for a family.

A company of one has nobody else to hold `recovery`. Its backup is the creator's own recovery phrase, and the create screen says so. When the second person joins, the Owner is asked who else should be able to use the kit.

A company is outside the one-family rule: an account can be in one family and any number of companies. The unique index on active family memberships already leaves `org` rows out.

### 3.2 Joining

**Invitation.** As in families (`groups-server.md` routes 11 to 17), with a role. An Admin lets the person in with one click: the admitting roster adds their entry and their employee area, and the call carries the area's base list, its custody seal, their grants (their own area, and every folder open to everyone) and the seat. Their device joins on first use. An invitation that gives `recovery`, or the Owner role, is let in by an Owner (2.1, rule 8).

**Extra emails on one account.** An account today has one address and no way to change it, and accepting an invitation needs the invited address. So a person with a personal account could not join as `sam@acme.com`.

- An account can add addresses (`account_emails`). Each is verified by a link mailed to it, opened while signed in to that account.
- An address belongs to one account. Adding one another account holds is refused without saying whose it is.
- **Only the primary address signs in and gets security mail.** Sign-in, the recovery mails, security notices and the 72-hour wait use the primary only. A work address added to a personal account can never be used to get into it. This matters: whoever runs acme.com's mail can read mail sent to `sam@acme.com`.
- **The primary can be changed.** Any verified address of the account can be made primary, after the password is confirmed (a one-use OPAQUE exchange tagged `emails`, as removing a device asks) and the second step where it is on. This answers `devices-design.md` 11's open question, and it is what someone needs who signed up with a work address and then leaves: their account, and the mail about it, should not stay at an address their former employer reads. On leaving a company, a person whose primary address is at one of its domains is asked to add another and switch.
    - **A change made with the second step passed is final at once.** The old address gets one notice that it is no longer the account's address, and nothing after. A password alone could not have made the change, and copies of a person's security mail sent for a month to a mailbox their former employer reads would be a leak of their own.
    - **A change made without a second step:** for 30 days security mail goes to both addresses, the new and the old, and the old address gets a link that undoes the change, good for 7 days (`account_email_changes`). It puts the old address back as primary and signs every device out. Without it, a stolen password on an account with no second step moves the account's security mail, and the 72-hour approval wait, to an address the thief reads, with one notice to show for it.
    - **The undo works once.** After an undo, the primary can be moved off that address again only with the second step on (409 `two-step-required`; the screen offers to set it up), and that change sends no undo link. So whoever reads the old mailbox has one veto, for at most a week, and not a fresh one at every attempt.
    - The cost that remains: someone leaving a company whose primary was a work address, with no second step on, can have the change undone once by whoever reads that work mailbox. The screen says so and offers the second step first.
- An invitation is accepted by an account that holds the invited address, primary or not.
- Removing an address removes nobody from anything. A domain decides who may ask, not who stays.

**Company email domains.**

- An Admin adds a domain on the web. The company proves it with a DNS TXT record, `hushos-verification=<token>`, on the domain or on `_hushos.<domain>`.
- The worker checks it at once and then daily (`groups.domains`). Three failed days in a row make it `lapsed`: it stops offering joins and auto-join, the Admins are told, and it verifies again when the record is back.
- A domain is verified for one company at a time. Public mail domains (gmail.com and the like, a fixed list) are refused.

**Join requests.** Someone whose account holds a verified address at a verified domain sees "Join Acme" at sign-up and in Account. Choosing it files a request.

- **A request is not a membership.** It is a row in `group_join_requests` and nothing else: no `group_memberships` row, no `accepted` state. The requester is served the company's name and domain (route C23) and whether their request is open. Every group route answers them `not-member`, so a mailbox at the domain never reads the member list or the chain. (An invitation still passes through `accepted`, as in families, because there an Admin chose the address.)
- It takes no seat and opens nothing. An Admin sees it with the requester's served identity (route C24) and lets them in as after an invitation, or declines. Letting in creates the membership as `active` in the admitting transaction; the newcomer's device then reads the chain and joins on first use.
- A declined request stays declined: that account, and the address it asked from, cannot ask again until an Admin clears it.
- Someone who was a member and left, or was removed, can ask again only as an ordinary request an Admin looks at. Their request is never automatic (below).

**Auto-join** (a policy, off by default; decision of 10 November).

With it on, a request from a verified domain is let in without anyone looking. An encrypted company still cannot admit anyone without a device that holds the group key, so "automatic" means an Admin's device does it unattended.

The switch is the roster's `rules` field (2.1), set by an Admin's signed roster. A device auto-admits only while the latest roster on its own accepted chain says so. The server's word that the policy is on counts for nothing.

1. The request is filed with `auto: true` only when all of these hold on the server: the domain is verified; the address was **proved within the last ten minutes** (a fresh link mailed to it and opened, `account_emails.proved_at`), so an address verified years ago on an account whose mailbox is long closed does not count; the account has no membership row in this company in any state, and no declined or barred request; and neither the address nor any other address the account holds is barred for this company (below).
2. The server lists it in `due.autoAdmit` for `members` holders. Any Admin's device that is unlocked and online takes it: a browser tab that is open, or a phone's background task (BGProcessingTask, WorkManager). It claims the request (a 5-minute lease) so two devices do not race.
3. **The device checks its own chain first.** It never auto-admits an account that has an entry on its accepted chain, in any state. So someone who left cannot walk back in whatever the server says about them.
4. It fetches the newcomer's served identity, checks its keys statement, and runs the ordinary admission as Member, on a roster whose `auto` field carries today's date by the device's clock and that day's count (2.1, rule 9): roster, employee area, base list, custody seal, grants of the folders open to everyone.
5. It never buys a seat. With no free paid seat the request waits as an ordinary one and the Admins are told.

Limits:

- Member only, and only folders open to everyone.
- At most `autoJoinDailyLimit` a day (10 unless an Admin changes it). The count is on the chain, signed, one roster after another, so it holds across every Admin's device and a server cannot spread requests between devices to get around it.
- Every member's app can tell an automatic admission from the roster itself and says "Dana joined Acme automatically". The member is marked "Joined automatically" on the People page until an Admin reviews them.
- Nobody is auto-admitted on a roster that owes a succession or a key replacement.
- **Barred addresses.** When someone is removed, leaves or is declined, the server bars the address they joined with and every address their account holds at the company's domains (`group_barred_addresses`). A request from a barred address, on any account, is never automatic, and the Admin who looks at it is told why: "This address belonged to Sam, who left on 3 November." An Admin clears an address once it has been given to someone new (route C24). The second draft barred the account only. A leaver whose company mailbox still worked for a few days could have moved the address to a fresh account, proved the mailbox, and walked back in. Addresses are compared with any `+tag` taken off the local part, so `sam+new@acme.com` is `sam@acme.com`. This is a **server rule**: the admitting device knows accounts from its chain, not addresses.

**What it costs.** With auto-join on, these people get into the company, its People list and every folder open to everyone, with nobody deciding:

- anyone who can read a mailbox at the domain today: the mail administrator, a catch-all address, a compromised inbox, someone given a former employee's address once an Admin has cleared it;
- **someone who left, for as long as the company's mail still reaches them** at an address that is not the barred one: a second name on their mailbox, a forwarding rule, a catch-all. The bar stops their own address and its `+tag` forms and nothing else. Closing a leaver's mailbox is the company's job, and with auto-join on the removal screen says so: "Close Sam's mailbox at acme.com, or turn automatic joining off.";
- whoever runs the HushOS server, which is the party that says an address was proved and that serves the newcomer's keys.

Without auto-join, every one of them still needs an Admin to click. With it they do not. It is confined and always visible, and it cannot be made safe. The policy's screen says this in plain words before it is turned on, and `hushos.com/security` states it.

### 3.3 Colleagues as contacts

- Every active entry on the company's accepted roster is listed in People under the company's name, and offered in the share picker, from the moment the device accepts the roster. No step.
- **The address shown is the one they joined with** (`group_memberships.joined_email`): the invited address, or the one a request was made from. Route 3 never serves an `org` member's primary address unless it is that one, so a personal address on a person's account does not appear in their employer's People list.
- They read as Not verified until the person checks them (a pair code inside the company, or twelve words).
- **Roster trust stays inside the company** (`groups-design.md` 2.8). In the company's folders a colleague's keys are the roster's. For sharing something from a personal space, the device writes a first-seen pin of them from their served identity when the share is made, as for any contact. The roster does not vouch for them there.
- **Colleagues are not written into the settings document.** A company's entries are read from the device's own verified chain, kept in its device record. Only a pin the person made themselves (a check, or a share from their personal space) goes into settings. A first-seen pin for each of 50 colleagues with churn would fill the 64 KiB document (`pins.ts`: "roughly a hundred people").
- When someone leaves, they drop off the list on the roster that marks them left. They stay in People if the person added them by hand or still shares something with them.
- A suspended member stays listed, marked "Paused".

### 3.4 Shared folders and employee areas

**Creating a shared folder.** An Admin names it and picks people (or everyone). Their device makes the area key and root, signs the base list, and sends route 21 with the roster row, the custody seal and the grants owed.

**Adding people.** A manager of the folder, or an Admin, signs an addition and sends it with the newcomer's grant (route C3). No roster. The next roster's `heads` commits it (2.3).

**Taking someone off, or lowering a role.** An Admin's device signs the roster row for the folder's next key version with the new base list, and sends the rotation as route 24 does. A manager who is not an Admin asks (route C4): the server stops serving the person at once and lists the folder in `due.rotations` for Admins.

**An Admin who is not on a folder** changes its keys through the company key, in a key-change session (3.6), which is recorded and is not browsing.

**Deleting a folder** is a tombstone row, as in families (2.10). An employee area is retired only after its assignee has left and its contents were handed over or deleted.

### 3.5 Sharing and its policies

Sharing in company folders uses 2.4's records. A company sets how its items may leave it. Families have no policies.

| Policy                                 | Options                                         | Default    | Enforced at                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------------- |
| Who can share company items            | Any editor; area assignees and data admins only | Any editor | `createShare`, `createLink` |
| Shares with people outside the company | Allowed; listed domains only; off               | Allowed    | `createShare`               |
| Public links                           | Allowed; off                                    | Allowed    | `createLink`, `resolveLink` |
| Link expiry ceiling                    | Days, or none                                   | 30 days    | `createLink`, `updateLink`  |
| Link password                          | Required; optional                              | Optional   | `createLink`, `updateLink`  |
| Expiry on shares with outside people   | Days, or none                                   | None       | `createShare`               |

- "Outside the company" means the grantee is not an active member. "Listed domains" compares the grantee's verified addresses.
- The server enforces each policy where the share or link is made or changed, and the apps hide what a policy forbids. Every policy in this table is a **server rule**. An app that ignores the policy is refused (403 `policy`, naming the rule).
- A walker also drops, at a key change, a share or link the policy no longer allows once its capped time has passed. It takes the server's policy row only as a reason to drop, never to keep (2.4).
- **Tightening.** When a policy gets stricter, the server lists the shares and links that no longer comply for the Admins, and caps each one's time from the change (`policy_expires_at`): to the new ceiling where there is one, or to 30 days where the policy is now "off". Nothing live is cut off silently, and an Admin can stop any of them sooner from the list, with a stop record.
- Every change is recorded in the ledger (`policy-changed`), made by an Admin.
- **What no policy stops.** Anyone who can open a file can download, copy or photograph it. Policies stop HushOS sharing on someone's behalf. They do not stop people, and no copy may suggest they do.

### 3.6 Offboarding

**Suspend** (reversible). An Admin pauses a member:

- `state = 'suspended'`. The server refuses every company route and every Drive route in the company's areas for them, on every device, at once (403 `suspended`). Their account and personal space are untouched: the account's sessions stay.
- Their devices show "Your access to Acme is paused", hide the space, and delete its catalogue and kept files. **Queued uploads and unsent drafts are kept,** sealed on the device, for 30 days: they are the person's unsaved work, and a pause is meant to be undone. An upload that was completing is refused, and its parts are swept as any unfinished upload's are.
- They keep their seat, their roster entry and the keys they hold. They are granted no new keys while paused and are not counted in "everyone" (2.3). Resuming restores access, fills the grants they lack and sends the queue.
- **Suspension changes no key.** It stops an honest server serving them. It is not a removal.
- A suspended person who held a role cannot use it. Suspending an Owner is for an Owner to do, and suspending the only Owner is refused.
- An Admin can pause another Admin. That is how an Admin who should go is stopped at once when no Owner is at hand to remove them (2.1, rule 12). It changes no key, and two Admins can pause each other; an Owner settles it.

**Remove.** An Admin's device removes a Member or a Billing holder. An Owner's removes an Admin, an Owner or someone who holds `recovery` (2.1, rules 8 and 12). In order:

1. **Hold** them (route 27), so they cannot write or start a key change meanwhile.
2. **The removal roster** (route 19): their entry marked `left`, with `heads` over every folder's list and share log as this device has verified them. That is what later refuses anything the leaver signs with an earlier date (2.3, "After someone leaves"). If they held `members`, the group key succession and its seals and escrow. If they held `data_admin`, the company key replacement (2.2). If they held `recovery`, a new kit as well (2.1, rule 7), which the Owner writes down before the call is sent. No folder rows. The server refuses the call when its heads for the leaver's folders are behind what it has stored (409 `heads-behind`, 2.3), and the device reads and signs again.
3. In that transaction the server:
    - ends their access, and deletes their grants, self-grants and list rows;
    - marks as owed the key change of every area they were entitled in by its list (all live areas, if they held `data_admin`);
    - **revokes every share granted to them** in the company's areas, by whoever, and makes each such node's subtree owed (2.4). They leave the company's files, not only its keys, as a family's leaver does. A colleague who still wants to share with them does it again, to an outsider, under the policy;
    - **pauses every share and link they made** (`paused_at`): it stops serving them until an Admin decides;
    - bars their addresses for automatic requests (3.2).
4. **Hand over** (route C12), on the screen that follows at once. The Admin chooses who takes their employee area (by default the Admin doing the removal), and for each thing they shared, **stop or keep. The default is stop.** Everything not kept is stopped by one signed batch stop per folder (2.4), so answering for a few hundred shares is a handful of entries, and none of it counts against anyone's budget. Keeping signs the next record as its new granter (2.4), which is accepted only for a record within the heads the removal committed, and the share is served again. A leaver's share to an address of their own is the obvious way to keep reading after leaving, which is why an Admin must look before any of them goes on (decision 10.10, reversed).
5. **The key changes.** The device works through the owed areas, at most 8 per roster: for each, a new key, root and base list without the person, the custody seal, the grants, then the Drive walk. Phones continue in the background. Whatever was not handed on by the time a folder's key changes is stopped by that walk, with the walker's stop record; so the screen does not let key changes start until each paused share has an answer.

**More than one Admin's device.** What is owed is the server's `due.rotations` and `due.subtrees`, checked against the device's own chain. A device claims a batch before it starts (route C31, a 10-minute lease it renews while it works), so two devices do not build the same roster and lose one to `stale`. A lapsed lease lets another device take over; the walk itself is resumable, as in families.

**Key-change sessions.** An Admin who is not on a folder reaches its key through the company key. For a folder that is owed a key change, the server serves them the rotation work in a key-change session (route C13, `purpose: 'key-change'`): the envelopes to re-seal, and no download URL. It is recorded in the ledger as `key-change`. It is not a browse session, writes no notice, and does not appear as "an admin opened this folder" in anyone's Info. The folder's people see the key change itself, from the roster row and its signer: "Maya, an admin, changed this folder's keys on 3 November." Walking a folder shows its file names to the Admin's device, which is why the session is in the ledger.

**While a key change is owed.** Uploads, new folders, copies and moves into that folder wait ("Finishing a key change…"), as they do during a family folder's walk; reading goes on. The server refuses them (409 `rotating`). The worker refuses them too, from its own chain and lists, never from the server's word (`areaOwed`, Rust `area_owed`). An area is owed a key change when the roster row of its current key is older than:

- the roster that marked `left` anyone its list (base or additions, as this device has verified them) names, or anyone at all where `everyone` is set;
- or the roster that last replaced the company key.

A subtree is owed one as 2.4 says.

**What that protects, exactly.** On a device that has verified the list the leaver was on, nothing is written under a key the leaver holds once the removal's roster is accepted. It does not hold:

- on a device the server kept an addition from. The addition's maker notices at the next roster (2.3, heads), and until an Admin acts the folder is not owed on anyone else's device;
- for outside editors, who hold no chain and rely on the server's refusal;
- against what the leaver already has: every key and file from before.

**What the leaver keeps.** Their account, their personal space and everything in it, their devices, and the contacts they added by hand. Their devices wipe the company's space on `not-member`, keeping the settings pin, as in families.

**What stays readable as theirs.** Their roster entry stays, marked `left`. Records they signed read as written by "Sam, who has left Acme", shown like Unverified. Re-keyed records keep their signature through every key change.

**Coming back.** Someone let in again before all their old folders' keys have changed gets no standing from the lists that named them before (2.3, "After someone comes back").

**Leaving by choice, and deleting an account.** What it takes depends on what the person holds. In every case the account is the person's, and nobody else's inaction keeps it in a company for more than 7 days.

- **A Member or a Billing holder** leaves as in a family: the departure is recorded, the server's part of step 3 runs at once, and the next roster, any Admin's, marks it.
- **An Owner, an Admin, and someone who only can use the kit give notice.** The roster that marks any of them left is an Owner's (2.1, rules 8 and 12), and none of them can sign it for themselves: it makes keys (a succession, a new company key, a new kit) that only someone who is still an Owner on that roster may sign. So "Leave Acme" from any of the three files a notice (route 20 answers `{ state: 'leaving', freeAt }`; `group_memberships.leaving_at`).
    - **Paused at once.** From the notice they are served nothing in the company and can use no role, as under a suspension and at their own request. It is the notice's own flag: an Admin's pause of the same person is a separate one (1.4), so withdrawing the notice lifts only the notice. Anyone who could use the kit is refused route C10 from that moment (a **server rule**).
    - **They keep their seat for the 7 days, and it is billed,** by the agreed seat rule: a seat counts until the person is removed. It is freed when the departure is recorded.
    - Every Owner is mailed, and reminded daily: "Dana is leaving Acme on 17 November. Remove them to change the keys they hold."
    - An Owner's device then removes them like anyone else (step 2 above); for an Owner who is leaving, another Owner's. A notice is not a departure: until then every roster lands as usual.
    - **The last Owner cannot give notice** (409 `owner`: "Make someone else an owner first"). An Owner who is paused or already under notice does not count as one who remains. That is the one refusal left, and it is in the person's own hands: giving the role is a roster they can sign.
    - **A last Owner with nobody to hand to closes the company.** When the last Owner is the company's only active member, their Leave, or their account's deletion, is not a notice: it dissolves the company at once, as a family's last member's does (`groups-design.md` 3.3: its folders are retired and purged 30 days later, and a live plan is cancelled first). A last Owner who has colleagues and no successor removes them first, which an Owner can always do. So this refusal never traps an account either.
- **After 7 days the person is free, whatever anyone did.** The departure is recorded, their membership ends, the seat is freed and they can delete their account.
    - **Who records it.** The worker's `groups.offboarding` job, every ten minutes, for each membership whose `leaving_at` is 7 days old; and the person's own next call (Leave again, or deleting their account), whichever comes first. Either runs the departure the family core already has (`depart`, `packages/db/src/groups.ts`), under its locks in its order: the `groups` row, then the areas the person was entitled in, in id order, then the membership row. It is idempotent, as the insert into `group_departures` already is.
    - **What it writes.** The membership as `left`, and a `group_departures` row with `held_members` as today and the new `needs_owner` set, since the person held `members` or `recovery`. It is set only in an `org`: a family's departures, and its `left-due`, are as they were. A family's blockers (`organizer`, `last-holder`) do not apply to an `org`: the one that matters, the last Owner, was refused at the notice.
- **A departure that waits for an Owner stops nothing.** In a family, no roster lands while someone who departed is still listed active (409 `left-due`). For a company that would hand a week-long notice a sting in its tail: the fourth draft kept that rule for a departed Admin, so that from day 8 nobody could be let in or removed and no folder made or re-keyed until an Owner acted, and any Admin could have set that off by giving notice while the only Owner was away. It protected nothing: through the notice week the same person held the same keys and rosters landed normally. So `left-due` never names a departure with `needs_owner`. Other Admins' rosters keep landing; `due.markLeft` lists the person for every Owner, with the daily mail.
    - **What it costs, plainly.** Until an Owner's roster marks them left, the departed person's entry still reads as an Admin (or an Owner, or a kit holder) on every device, **and every key they held is still the current one:** the group key, the company key, the kit. An honest server serves them nothing and takes nothing from them, exactly as in the notice week. A server working with them could still pass a roster or a record they sign to members' devices, exactly as it could for a paused Admin. Nothing new is owed a key change for someone who held the company key until that roster lands: a folder key changed before the company key is replaced would be sealed to a key the leaver still holds.
    - **What the company's page shows meanwhile.** To Owners: "Dana has left Acme. Finish removing them: until you do, the keys Dana held are still in use." To Admins: "Dana has left. Waiting for an owner to finish removing them." To members, on People: "Left", beside the role the roster still shows.
    - **For someone who only could use the kit,** the server's part of step 3 runs at the departure as for a Member, and any Admin's device changes the keys of the folders they were on. The kit they may have kept is still the company's kit until an Owner makes a new one: the Owners are told "Sam left and could use the recovery kit. Make a new one.", and it stays in `due.kit` until it is done.
    - **With no Owner left to act,** that is what the recovery kit is for: the recovery roster is exempt from `left-due` altogether (C29), and the recovered Owner marks whoever has gone.
- **Deleting an account.** For a Member or a Billing holder it departs first, as in a family. For someone who would have to give notice it does not jump the notice, and it does not make them come back in a week either:
    - the deletion is authorised as it is today (the password exchange, and the second step where it is on), and is then recorded as pending (`users.deletion_pending_at`) instead of carried out;
    - it files the notice in every company where the person has not given one (the last Owner of a company is refused here as at Leave, before anything of theirs is cancelled or stopped);
    - the account goes on working outside those companies, and the person is mailed that it will be deleted on the day the last notice runs out, with a link that cancels it. Signing in and cancelling also withdraws the notices this deletion filed;
    - the worker completes the deletion when the last notice has run out, recording each departure first, then doing what an immediate deletion does (`beforeDelete`, which stops billing, then `deleteAccount`).
- The third draft refused Leave to everyone who held `recovery` until an Owner took the role back. A Member given only "can use the kit" cannot sign a roster, so if the Owner never acted, that person could never have left or deleted their account. The fourth draft made that a notice and told an Owner to "step down first" on their own roster, which an Owner cannot sign either (1.2): one whose fellow Owner did nothing could never have left.
- An account deleted by an operator, or an Owner whose account is simply lost, is the case the recovery roster's route covers.
- Company areas never go with a deleted account: `deleteAccount` deletes only personal workspaces.

**The server's part** is a pg-boss job (`groups.offboarding`): reminders to Admins while key changes or hand-overs are owed, and an operator notice past 7 days; the daily mail to Owners while someone is under notice or has left and is still to be marked; recording a departure when its notice runs out; and completing an account deletion that was waiting for one.

### 3.7 The admin browser

- **Always there** for data admins. There is no switch to turn it off, because a switch nobody flips protects nobody.
- **Shown.** Every company folder and employee area says so in its Info: "Admins of Acme can open this folder." A person's first visit to their company space says it once, in a sentence.
- **Opening.** The admin's device fetches the folder's custody seal (route C5, itself a ledger fact), opens the company seed from its own seal, then the folder's key, and checks it against the roster's commitment.
- **A session.** To be served a folder they are not on, the device opens a browse session (route C13) with a **statement it signs**, which the server checks and seals for the folder's people (below). Drive serves a data admin an area on the browsing basis only with a live session id; without one it answers as to a stranger. A session ends after 30 minutes without a request, and after 4 hours whatever happens (`BROWSE_SESSION_MAX_SECONDS`). Going on needs a new session, a new signed statement and so a new notice: one notice never stands for weeks of reading (decision 10.49).
- **In the admins' ledger.** The session, and every recorded request inside it, are ledger facts with basis `admin-browse`. They do not depend on the admin's app reporting anything: an app that cached a folder's key still has to ask the server for every listing and every file.
- **What an admin can do there:** list, open, download, upload, rename, move, trash, restore, delete forever, change the folder's people, change its keys. Everything they write is signed as themselves.
- **What they cannot:** share or make a link there. A share record is signed by its granter, so nobody shares "as" someone else. An admin who needs to share adds themselves to the folder, which its people see.
- An admin who is on a folder's list is not browsing there. They are one of its people, their reads are ordinary member facts in the ledger, and no notice is written (9.9).

**The notice.** The folder's people cannot read the admins' ledger, and the server keeps nothing readable to show them. The second draft had the admin's own app seal them a note under the folder's key. A modified app could seal junk, and the folder's people then knew that an admin had been there and not which one. So the note is now sealed by the server, to keys only the folder's people can open, around a statement the admin's device signs and the server checks before it opens anything.

```
noticeSeed(v) = HKDF-SHA-256(ikm = areaKey(v), salt = 32 zero bytes, info = "hushos/group/browse-notice/v2" ‖ groupId ‖ workspaceId, length = 96)
              = X25519 seed (32) ‖ ML-KEM-768 seed (64): the folder's notice keys at key version v
statement     = ["hushos/group/browse-open",1,groupId,workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,sessionId,adminUserId,adminKeyVersion,rosterVersion,rosterDigest,startedAt]
notice        = [statement, signature, at]
sealed        = ephemeral X25519 public key (32) ‖ ML-KEM ciphertext (1,088) ‖ nonce (24) ‖ XChaCha20-Poly1305(notice, ad = ["hushos/group/browse-notice-seal",2,groupId,workspaceId,workspaceKeyVersion,seq])
```

- **The notice keys.** Whoever makes or changes a folder's key derives its notice keys and sends the two public keys with that roster call (`noticeKeys`, kept in `area_notice_keys`). Everyone who holds the folder key derives the same pair and compares. A mismatch shows on the folder ("This folder's admin notices can't be read. Change its keys.") and names whoever made that key, as a bad custody seal does. The server refuses a call that makes a folder key without them (`invalid` (`notice-keys`)).
- **Opening a session.** The admin's device signs the statement, hybrid, under its identity. The server verifies that signature under the admin's roster entry, as it does a seal's, and opens no session without one that holds. It then seals the statement, the signature and its own clock (`at`) to the folder's notice keys, as it seals a ledger epoch key (HKDF `info` `hushos/group/browse-notice-seal/v2`; no signature, since the server has no identity), numbers the notice in the area (`area_notices.seq`), and keeps the sealed bytes and nothing else. It cannot read one back.
- A member's device opens it with the notice keys, verifies the admin's signature under the roster, and shows it in the folder's Info: "Maya, an admin, opened this folder on 3 November at 14:02." It keeps the highest number it has seen and notes a gap. A statement names its session and carries the admin's own clock (`startedAt`): a session id seen before is shown once, and a statement whose own time is more than 10 minutes from the server's reads "the times don't match". So an old statement cannot be sealed again as a new visit.
- **What it proves.**
    - **Who:** the admin's own signature, under the keys their roster entry names. The server alone cannot make it, and a modified admin app cannot avoid it, because no session opens without one that verifies. Another Admin cannot make it, with the server's help or without: an Admin's keys change only on their own roster or an Owner's (2.1, rule 8). An Owner can write other keys into an Admin's entry and sign as them; that roster is on every device, signed by the Owner who did it.
    - **When:** the server's clock.
    - **That every session has a notice:** a **server rule**. A hostile server can open a session and write no notice. No design that keeps the server from reading notices prevents that. What it cannot do is write one in the name of an admin who did not sign, or pass an earlier visit off as a new one.
- Counts of what was downloaded are in the admins' ledger and are not shown to members.
- **Through a key change.** The rotation carries `previousNoticeSeed`: the old notice seed wrapped under a key derived from the new one (136 bytes: a 24-byte nonce, the seed and a 16-byte tag). The wrapping key is `HKDF-SHA-256(ikm = noticeSeed(toVersion), salt = 32 zero bytes, info = "hushos/group/browse-notice-key/v2", length = 32)`, the cipher XChaCha20-Poly1305, and the associated data `["hushos/group/browse-notice-key",2,groupId,workspaceId,toVersion,fromVersion]`, when the area has notices. So someone who holds only the current folder key reads earlier notices by walking back. It gives a removed member nothing (they hold no new key) and gives a new member no old folder key (a notice seed is derived one way). The rotator opens it before sending, as it does the previous-root envelope; a bad one makes earlier notices unreadable to members and changes nothing in the ledger.

**Residual.** An admin keeps what they read. Disclosure and a record do not prevent reading; they make it known.

### 3.8 Activity and alerts

**What is recorded.** Each of these requests in a company area writes a sealed fact (2.5). Those marked with a record are refused from a member's device without a valid detail record (400 `audit`):

| Action                                                                                                                  | When                                                                                                                                              | Record                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `download`                                                                                                              | A file version's download URL is issued, each time one is issued. The batch route (up to 100 URLs, for a zip) takes one record per version        | Yes                                                                              |
| `read`                                                                                                                  | The thumbnails route returns URLs: one fact per call, naming every version id in it                                                               | Yes: one for the call, over the digest of its ids                                |
| `upload`, `new-folder`                                                                                                  | An upload completes; a folder is made                                                                                                             | Yes                                                                              |

| `trash`, `restore`, `delete-forever`                                                                                    | The Drive call                                                                                                                                    | Yes                                                                              |
| `share`, `share-changed`, `share-stopped`, `link`, `link-changed`, `link-stopped`                                       | A share log entry is stored                                                                                                                       | Yes                                                                              |
| `link-opened`                                                                                                           | Someone resolves a link                                                                                                                           | No: the visitor has no account                                                   |
| `people-changed`                                                                                                        | A folder list addition or a new base list                                                                                                         | Yes                                                                              |
| `custody-seal-read`, `browse-opened`, `browse-ended`, `key-change`                                                      | A data admin's sessions                                                                                                                           | Yes                                                                              |

| `storage-moved`                                                                                                         | A move of the company's files ends, or is blocked                                                                                                 | No: the server's own                                                             |
| `device-opened`, `device-waiver`                                                                                        | A device opens the company's space for the first time; a waiver is given or used                                                                  | Yes                                                                              |
| `alert-fired`                                                                                                           | A rule fires                                                                                                                                      | No: the server's own                                                             |
| `report`                                                                                                                | Someone reports an item in a company area to the operator of the service                                                                          | Yes when the reporter is a member; none from someone outside or a link's visitor |
| `report-read`                                                                                                           | An operator fetches a reported item's key or its bytes                                                                                            | No: the operator is not a member                                                 |

- **People outside the company.** A request by someone a file was shared with from outside, or by a link's visitor, writes a fact with no record and is not refused for lacking one (2.5). Those rows are the server's word and the page says so.
- **Reports hand the operator a key.** Reporting an item to whoever runs the service (`createReport`) seals that item's key to the operators, who can then fetch its bytes (`reportDownloadUrls`). In a company's area that is a company file leaving to HushOS, or to the self-hoster's operators, on one member's say. It stays allowed: it is how abuse is reported, and a company must not be able to switch it off for its own files. But it is never silent: filing one writes a `report` fact, an operator's every fetch writes `report-read`, the report screen says "This sends the file's key to the people who run HushOS", and an alert rule can match either (decision 10.48). Section 5 counts it. **Most reporters are not members:** someone a file was shared with from outside, or a link's visitor, has no activity key (2.5), so their `report` fact carries no record and is the server's word, as their other requests are. A member's carries their record, and is refused without one.
- **A reissued URL is a new download.** An app reuses a download URL for its 15 minutes, resuming and reading ranges as it likes. Asking for a new one writes a new fact and needs a new record.
- **Thumbnails.** In the current content suite a file's thumbnail is a trailer of the same object as its contents, and the thumbnails route returns a URL for the whole object, a hundred at a time (`drive-design.md`, "Fetching thumbnails"). So it is a way to download everything. The first draft counted these reads per hour, which would have let a modified app take a whole folder and leave a number. Every version id it returns is now in a fact. Those facts are many (opening a folder of pictures writes one, naming each picture), carry one record for the whole call (2.5), and are kept three months in their own table (`group_read_events`), not twelve.
- **Retention.** Twelve months for everything else, dropped as whole monthly partitions by the migration role's job, with their epoch keys. The application's database role has INSERT and SELECT on the ledger tables, the worker UPDATE on `link_n` alone (2.5), and nobody DELETE.

**What the server keeps readable: nothing of this.** The fact is sealed before it is stored (2.5). The time, the actor, the device, the action, the item, the basis and the member's record are all inside. A stored row shows the company, the month, a row id, which ten-minute key sealed it and which link covers it. What Drive's own tables still show about writes is in section 5.

**Reading.** An Admin's device (web, and read-only on phones) pages through the rows, opens each epoch key with the company key, opens the facts, opens each device's activity key, opens the details, verifies the chain's links, and shows names. Each row is shown as 2.5's table says: corroborated by the person's device, the server's word, the app's word, or with what is odd about its record or its time.

**What stays the app's word.**

- **Why bytes were read.** The server knows it issued a download URL. Whether the person opened the file, saved it, or kept it offline is in the detail, and a modified app can say `open` for everything.
- **Kept offline, opened while offline, exported to another app, printed.** No request reaches the server when these happen. The app reports them at its next sync (route C16, `reported`). A modified app reports nothing.
- **Copying out of the company.** A copy inside the company is a server call. A copy to a personal space is a download here and an upload there, which the server must not link (the personal space is private). The app's reason says `copy-out`.
- **Screens.** Nothing sees a photograph of a screen.

The activity page states these in a "What this log can and can't show" note, and sales copy must not call it proof of what someone did with a file.

**Export.** CSV or JSON, built on the Admin's device from the decrypted rows, on the web, with the chain link it was verified to. The export itself is a fact.

**Alerts.** An Admin sets rules on the web:

| A rule can match                                        | Example                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Actions                                                 | Any `download`, `delete-forever`, `share` |
| A person, or anyone                                     | Sam                                       |
| A folder, by id, with everything under it               | Anything in "Finance"                     |
| A count in a window of at most 24 hours                 | 100 downloads or reads in 10 minutes      |
| Shares with people outside the company; public links    | Any                                       |
| Admin browsing                                          | Any session, or a session on one folder   |
| A device opening the company's space for the first time | Any                                       |

- A rule's conditions are ids, enums and numbers, which the server must read to apply them. Its name is sealed to the company key.
- A rule cannot match a file's name or type: the server never has them.
- "A new device" is about the company's space only. A company is never told when a person signs in to their own account elsewhere.
- **Evaluation happens in the request,** while the server holds the fact it is about to seal. It cannot look back: what it stored it cannot read. A rule with no count fires at once. A rule with a count adds to a counter.
- **A counter** (`group_alert_counters`) holds: the rule, the window's start, the person (only where the rule counts per person), the count so far, whether it has fired, and the ids of the first and last sealed rows it counted.
    - **Increments are batched.** A process adds to its counts in memory and writes them every five seconds, or at once when one of them reaches its rule's number, as one upsert per counter. Writing on every request would put one row lock in front of every download in the company for a rule that counts everyone, which is the company-wide lock the chain was moved to the worker to avoid. The price: a crash loses at most five seconds of counting, and a rule can fire up to five seconds late.
    - The upsert returns the new total. The rule fires when the total first reaches its number, once per window (`fired`).
    - It is deleted when its window closes, by a sweep every minute, and a window is at most 24 hours.
    - **What a copy of the database shows while one exists:** that a rule's count stands at some number since the window's start, and for a per-person rule whose count it is. One rule that counts each person's downloads over 24 hours is therefore a rolling one-day tally of every member's downloads, readable in the database. It is the one readable trace of reading this design keeps, for at most a day, and only for rules a company chose to set. The rule's screen says so before it is saved.
- **A firing** is itself a sealed fact (`alert-fired`, naming the rule and the range of rows) and a notification event of kind `company-alert` (class `security`) for each data admin, through the existing path (`packages/db/src/schema/notifications.ts`, the push relay and Web Push). The push carries only an event id. The admin's device fetches the firing row, opens it and the rows it names with the company key, and writes the words itself: "Sam downloaded 214 files from Clients in 10 minutes." A locked device shows the generic line.
- The notification event, kept 30 days as every notification event is (`NOTIFICATION_RETENTION_DAYS`), shows that an alert fired for a company at a time, and to whom. Not which rule, and not why.
- **Email.** The server cannot write the words, so the mail says only that an alert set in Acme fired and to open HushOS.
- An Owner without `data_admin` does not exist (1.2), so every recipient can read the detail.

### 3.9 Security policies

**Require the second step.** As `two-step-design.md` has it ("When a policy requires the step"): account-wide, enrolment at the next sign-in, "Required by Acme", and nobody but the person can turn off a step they chose. A person in two companies needs it if either asks. When they leave, the requirement goes and their step stays until they turn it off.

**Require approval of new devices.** Level 1 only ("Approve new devices"). Level 2 ("Keys need a device") is not built.

`devices-design.md` 4 has a company policy switch the level on for the member's whole account. This design narrows it: **a company's policy gates the company's space, per device, not the account** (decision 10.2).

- **Rows are written from the first day, policy or not.** Every device that opens the company's areas gets a `group_device_approvals` row when it first does: `first` for the first device a member ever opens the company on, `open` for a later one that arrived while the policy was off. So turning the policy on later never makes the next device to arrive a "first" one, whoever is holding it.
- With the policy on, a device may open the company's areas only if it has such a row for that company and is not a removed device.
- **Turning the policy on.** Devices that already have a row keep opening the company. The Admin who turns it on is shown each member's devices and how each came to be there, and can remove any; a removed device needs approval like a new one. From then on a device without a row needs approval.
- **The first device** a member ever opens the company on is approved as `first`: there is nothing to approve it from. So the policy applies only once the member has registered a device, as the owner asked.
- **Every device after that** needs approval, for good. The gate is keyed on whether the member has **ever** had a row for this company, not on whether a device is signed in now: rows are kept when a device signs out (signing out revokes the device and keeps its row, `signOutDevice`). The first draft let a member who had signed out everywhere start again ungated, which made the policy void for anyone with one browser.
- A later device signs in to the account as usual (password, and the second step if on). The company's space shows "Approve this device to open Acme". It is approved:
    - from another of the person's devices that opens the company now, with the committed code of `devices-design.md` 2 (typed or scanned), recorded as `device`;
    - or with the person's recovery phrase, recorded as `phrase`;
    - or by a one-time waiver, below.
- **What that means for someone with one device.** A browser that signs out deletes its local data, so signing in again is a new device. A member who uses only the web and signs out needs their recovery phrase or an Admin's waiver to open the company again. The policy's screen says so before it is turned on.
- The second-step code does not stand in for approval. If it did, this policy would be "require the second step" under another name (decision 10.3).
- There is no 72-hour email wait here. The waiver replaces it.
- It is a **server rule**, like level 1 everywhere: it holds against a stolen password at an honest server and not against the server.
- **No admin ever approves a member's device.** Approval comes from the person's own device or phrase. The waiver below is not an approval: it lifts the company's own requirement once.
- A person's own choice to approve new devices on their account (`devices-design.md` 2) is separate, off by default, and nothing a company or admin can turn on, off or waive.

**The one-time waiver.** For a member whose other devices are gone and who has no phrase at hand:

- **It is given for one request.** The member's new device has already asked to be approved (route C20). That request shows a short code, the device's name and its rough location. The member reads the code to an Admin, who finds that request and waives it (route C19). The waiver approves that request's device and no other, and lapses with the request, 24 hours at most. The second draft's waiver went to whichever device arrived first within a day, which could have been an attacker's.
- It can be given only for a member who has ever had a row for the company, so it cannot open the space for an account that never opened it. (Such an account needs none: its first device is `first`.)
- The request's device is then approved as `waiver` and the waiver is spent.
- It lifts only this company's gate. It changes nothing about the person's account, their own approval setting, or another company.
- It is a ledger fact when given and when used, and a security notice to every device of the member and to every other Admin.

**What a waiver is worth to an attacker.** Someone holding a member's password (and second step) who talks an Admin into a waiver opens the company's space on their own device. The notices are the control, and the People page shows the member's devices for the company with how each was approved.

### 3.10 Spaces and the switcher

One account holds its personal space and any number of companies.

- **Personal** stays as shipped: My files, with the family as a place inside it (#395). Its URLs do not change (`/app/…`).
- **Each company is its own space.** On the web its URL is `/app/s/<groupId>/…`, and the last space used opens first.
- **The switcher** sits at the top of the sidebar: the person's own name for Personal, then each company by name. It is not shown to someone in no company.

| Client  | Switching                                                                                              | What is per space                                     |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Web     | The switcher; the space in the URL                                                                     | Home, Recents, search, tags, Trash, storage numbers   |
| iOS     | A picker in the top bar; one Files location per space, each tied to its owner (account, server, space) | The Files location, kept files, transfer queues       |
| Android | The same picker; one provider root per space                                                           | The provider root, offline copies, the transfer queue |

- **Inside a company's space:** My Acme files (the employee area), Shared folders (those the person is on), Shared with me (items colleagues shared), People, and for Admins: Members, Activity, Settings.
- **Search** looks in the current space, with an "All spaces" option that merges each area's catalogue on the device. The server never sees a query.
- **Moving between spaces** is copy then trash, through the device, every version. A copy out of a company is recorded there (3.8).
- **Leaving or removal** takes the space out of the switcher and wipes its local state on every device. **Suspension** hides it and keeps the unsent queue (3.6).
- **API.** `GET /api/drive/spaces` already lists group spaces. For a company it lists only the areas the person is entitled in, each with its kind, list head and role, and its `space` gains `groupKind: 'org'`. `/api/drive/capabilities` gains `companies`.
- **Apps from before this release.** A shipped app reads `{ kind: 'group' }` as a family and would show a company as a family that fails its checks. So the server lists `org` spaces, and answers `org` group routes, only for a client at or above the company floor: a company-write number the web sends as it sends the family-write number (`group-write-floor.ts`), and a minimum app version for the phones. Anything below gets no company space and 426 `unsupported` on its routes, with "Update HushOS to open Acme".

**Phones.** Each PR in section 8 covers web, iOS and Android (and the Rust core) together. The phones get every member feature and people management: invitations, letting people in, roles, suspending, removing with its key changes, folders and their people, the admin browser, approving a device, and reading Activity. These are on the web only: domains, policies, alert rules, the activity export, and (in `docs/company-storage-design.md`) the bucket. Billing is read-only on phones, as for families.

### 3.11 Limits at 50 seats

The family design assumes every member opens every folder. Three of its choices do not survive a company:

| Family rule                                                                                                                                                      | What breaks                                                                    | Company rule                                                                                                                                        | What it costs                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| At most `GROUP_AREA_LIMIT` areas (default 10, never above 50, `GROUP_AREA_LIMIT_MAX`), because every roster call makes room for every area's workspace documents | 50 people need 50 employee areas before the first shared folder                | `ORG_AREA_LIMIT`, default 500 live areas, counted under the group lock. A roster call makes room for the documents of at most 8 areas (2.1, rule 6) | About 21 MiB of documents allowance per call at the cap, in line with a family's at ten areas. A removal's key changes take several calls                                                                             |
| A removal rotates every area in its own roster                                                                                                                   | One roster cannot carry 500 key changes, and most folders are not the leaver's | The removal roster carries none. The areas the leaver could open are owed, and uploads there wait (3.6)                                             | A window in which the leaver still holds keys. An honest server serves them nothing and nothing new is written under those keys on devices that saw the list. Folders stay read-only until an Admin's device finishes |
| Every roster with changes locks all the group's areas                                                                                                            | Each admission or role change would pause Drive writes across the company      | A company roster locks the group row, then only the areas its rows touch. A departure locks the areas the person was entitled in                    | The departure of someone on many folders still pauses those folders for the write                                                                                                                                     |

**Lock order,** for the routes the family contract does not have: the `groups` row first, as always, for every roster route, the recovery roster (C29), suspension (C26) and a claim (C25, C31); then the `workspaces` rows touched, in id order; then list rows, the share log and membership rows; then storage. A list addition (C3), a narrowing request (C4) and a share log append take only the area's `workspaces` row and read the signer's membership under it, as a Drive write does (`requireStillWriter`); a departure takes the same row for every area the leaver was entitled in, so an addition cannot land beside the removal of its signer. The heads checks of 2.3 read each area's stored list and share log head: under that area's lock for the areas a roster's rows touch, which is what makes "no key change over a missing stop" exact, and without one for the rest. A departure recorded when a notice runs out takes the locks a Leave takes (3.6). The sealed ledger takes no lock a request waits on: a fact is an insert, the chain is the worker's under an advisory lock of its own, and counters are written in batches (2.5, 3.8).

Other numbers:

- **Seats.** `ORG_SEAT_LIMIT_DEFAULT` is 50. `groups.seat_limit` overrides it per company. An operator sets it from the management area (`/app/admin`, a new Companies page beside Affiliates), which is recorded. Above the limit the company's page says to get in touch.
- **Rosters.** A company writes more of them than a family: one per admission, role change, folder creation and batch of key changes. The family limits (60 an hour, 500 a day) stay for those. **Key changes that are owed get a budget of their own** (`groups:roster-owed`, 240 an hour and 3,000 a day, for a roster that carries only rows of areas the server lists as owed and changes no entry, and for a roster that changes nothing but `heads`, 2.3). Without it, removing a data admin at 500 folders is 63 rosters against 60 an hour, and nobody could be let in until the next day's work was done.
- **Roster size.** `members` as `["same", …]` (2.1) keeps a folder's or a key change's roster to a few kilobytes. Only rosters that change a person carry the full list, about 40 KB at 50 seats.
- **Reading heads.** A signer needs every folder's head before each roster. Route C34 returns them all in one page per 500 folders, with what changed since a roster version, so signing a roster at 500 folders is one or two reads, not a thousand against a 300-a-minute limit.
- **Share log entries** have limits of their own (2.4): 20 records a share, 500 live shares and links a member, 1,000 entries a member a day. Stops by a `members` holder or a walker, batch stops and hand-over records count against none.
- **A new device** verifies the chain from roster 1. One ML-DSA-65 verification measured 3.0 ms in Bun on a current Mac; at two hybrid verifications and a parse per roster, 3,000 rosters are about 30 seconds there and several times that on a slow phone. It runs in the background behind "Checking Acme's history", once per device. It must be measured on the slowest supported phone before the switch-on. If it is past a minute at 3,000 rosters, the remedy is the core's to design (a checkpoint signed by an Owner), not this document's, and the switch-on waits for it.
- **The company's pin** in the settings document holds the first keys and the head (version, digest, key digest) and **no map of areas.** A family's pin lists each area's version, about 45 bytes each; at 500 folders that alone is a third of the document. An area's version is on the chain the head already protects, and each device keeps its per-area marks in its device record.
- **The chain cache** the server rewrites on every chain write (`groups-server.md`, "Still open") grows with the areas a chain has listed. It gets a version for format 3 and must store statements by reference before companies are switched on.
- **Body caps.** Route 17 gains room for the newcomer's employee area (its root, list and custody seal: 64 KiB), which a family admission never carries. Roster routes gain 128 bytes per changed heads row, and 1.7 KiB per folder key they make, for its notice keys.
- **Minimum.** Plans set a minimum seat count; this design needs none.

## 5. What the server learns

Three lists, because they are different kinds of knowledge. The first two are what this design adds. The third is what Drive's tables already hold in every space, which a claim about "no history" has to count.

**What it must read, to refuse people or to bill.** It keeps these, readable, for as long as the company exists:

- the company's name. Invitation mails and invoices carry it;
- who is in the company, the address each joined with, and their state;
- who holds which role, who is a data admin, who can use the kit;
- which folders the company has, each one's kind and assignee, and which accounts are on each folder's list with which role. By account and folder id, never by a folder's name;
- the share log of each folder: which account shared which item id with which account or link, and that it was stopped. In order, with no time on an entry;
- the company key's and the kit's public keys (never a private half);
- the company's verified email domains, each account's extra addresses, join requests, and the addresses barred from automatic ones;
- the policies, and whether auto-join is on (from the roster);
- the seat count and the plan;
- its alert rules' conditions (not their names);
- which member certified an activity key, and in which month. No device id;
- which of a member's devices open the company's space, how each came to, and on which day;
- changes the two-person rule is holding, and requests to use the kit, with who approved or objected;
- who gave notice to leave, and when; who has left and is still to be marked; and, on the account, a deletion that is waiting for a notice to run out;
- forks devices reported, by the two digests, and who dismissed each;
- where its files are kept (`company-storage-design.md` section 12): its region; its own bucket's address, the location the company states for it, and a key to it sealed under a key the database does not hold; its export target, schedule and pruning; its retention settings, and a pending change with its date; a move's state and counts; when each of its stores last passed a test, to the hour; when each export run started and finished, and how much it copied; how many of its objects are missing. No account id is on any of these. Who changed them is in the ledger (3.8);
- **reports.** An item in a company area that someone reported to the operators is theirs to open: the report holds that item's key sealed to them, as in every space (`drive_reports`). That is a company file readable by whoever runs the service, by one member's choice. It is recorded in the company's ledger when filed and each time it is read (3.8).

**What it only handles in passing, and does not keep readable:**

- **each request in a company area:** which account asked for which item id, for what, when. It has to see that to serve it. It seals its fact of it to the company's key before storing it, and ten minutes later uses no key to what it wrote (2.5);
- **a rule's running count,** for windows still open, at most 24 hours, and for a per-person rule whose count it is (3.8);
- **that an alert fired** for the company, to whom and when, for 30 days. Not which rule;
- **a data admin's live session:** who, on which folder, until it ends. The row is deleted then; the notice it leaves is sealed to the folder's people (3.7);
- **the shape of the ledger:** how many sealed rows a company has in each month, under each ten-minute key and each link, in order. No time finer than the month. A notice row has a number and no time.

**What Drive's own tables show, in a company's areas as in every space.** These are not new, they are not sealed, and a copy of the database has them (`packages/db/src/schema/drive.ts`):

**What this design stops writing in a company's areas,** because these two are read history and nothing else:

| Column                       | Today                                                                | In a company's areas               | What is lost                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `drive_objects.last_read_at` | Set at most hourly when a download URL is issued (`touchObjectRead`) | Never written                      | Tiering (`tierObjects`) goes by age alone there: an object moves to the cold class 90 days after it was stored whether or not it is read, and does not come back by being read. hushos.com sets no cold class today (Garage and B2 have none), so nothing changes there |
| `drive_links.last_used_at`   | Set at most once a minute when a link is opened (`touchLink`)        | Never written                      | Nothing a person sees: no client shows it. The once-a-minute limit on counting moves into the process's memory                                                                                                                                                          |
| `drive_links.use_count`      | Counted with it                                                      | **Still counted** (decision 10.39) | Kept, because the link's maker sees "Opened 3 times" and is not an Admin with the ledger. **What it never has:** any file's or folder's name, contents, type or exact size beyond today's padding; what a detail record or a browse notice says; an alert rule's name; the company seed, the group seed or the kit's secret; any personal space's contents or activity. Nothing about a personal space is written to any company's ledger.

**The limits, stated once.**

- A server that serves files sees who asks for them. Sealing means a copy of the database, a backup, or the server a week later holds no history of who **read** what. It does not stop a dishonest server that is running from writing its own notes as requests arrive.
- The write columns above are readable. Moving them inside the sealed metadata is a change to Drive in every space, not to companies, and is decision 10.38.
- A database kept with point-in-time recovery archives the commit time of every insert, sealed rows included (2.5).
- `hushos.com/security` and the privacy policy say all of this when it ships. The sentence they may use is "HushOS keeps no record it can read of who opened or downloaded a company's files", not "no activity history".

## 7. Tests that must exist

Per AGENTS.md, everything here that handles keys, money or quota gets a Vitest suite beside it, and the Rust core its own with the shared fixtures. Every test must fail on a defect someone would notice.

**Crypto (TS and Rust, shared fixtures, added to `groups-invariants.md` with the checks they map to):**

- roster format 3: accepted only for `org` and `org` only at format 3; a format 2 statement never verifies as format 3; `["same", digest]` accepted only when the digest is the previous roster's members and never at version 1, and refused on a roster that changes an entry; the `custody` field's forward-only rule; a repeated company or kit key refused; an area's kind changing refused; `guardian` refused;
- a roster that drops a data admin without replacing the company key is refused, by `nextRoster` and by acceptance;
- **the kit rule:** a roster that takes `recovery` from an entry, or marks a `recovery` holder left, without a new kit is refused; a new kit without a new company key is refused;
- **the Owner rule:** an Admin's roster that marks an Owner left, demotes an Owner, gives or takes `recovery`, adds an entry that holds `recovery`, changes the keys or key version of an Owner's or a `recovery` holder's entry (a fold-in included), or raises `kitVersion`, is refused; the same roster signed by an Owner is accepted; an Owner's fold-in of their own new identity, signed under their previous keys, is accepted; an Admin still removes a Member and makes a Member an Admin; **planted keys, end to end:** an Admin writes keys of their own into an Owner's entry, and neither that roster nor one signed "as the Owner" under those keys is accepted; a roster that keeps `kitVersion` with other kit keys is refused;
- **new keys are an Owner's (rule 12):** an Admin's roster that raises `custodyKeyVersion`, and so one that removes or demotes an Admin or marks a departed Admin left, is refused, and the same roster from an Owner is accepted; a succession of an `org` group signed by an Admin is refused, while the same shape for a family is accepted; **the shut-out, end to end:** an Admin holding the group seed cannot produce a succession or a company key replacement that any device accepts, with or without seals to the Owners;
- a `left` entry that names a role is refused, and after the roster that marked a kit holder left, later rosters need not raise `kitVersion` again;
- a kit signature made under the group's ML-DSA context, or a group signature under the kit's, does not verify;
- **a guarded fold-in:** `nextRoster` refuses to fold a new identity into an Owner's or a kit holder's entry for another Owner without the fresh check, and builds it for that person's own roster; a link signed only by the old keys, served to another Owner's device, is not followed into a guarded entry;
- `members` without `data_admin`, and no Owner, are refused;
- **auto-join:** a device auto-admits only under a roster on its own chain whose `rules` say so; a served "on" below the device's marks is a rollback; an `auto` roster with a second change, a role, a day earlier than the last one's, a count that skips or repeats, or a count above the limit is refused; two devices that each sign "count 3" cannot both land; a device refuses to auto-admit an account that has any entry on its chain;
- **the recovery roster:** accepted from a `recovery` holder for exactly their own promotion to Owner, with the kit's signature; refused with any second change, a change to the signer's keys, any area row, another `custody`, `rules` or `heads`, a non-empty `auto`, from someone without `recovery`, or under another group key; **refused without `kitSignature`, with one made by another kit, by an earlier kit version, or over another roster version or statement; an Admin who holds the group seed and a `recovery` holder's identity keys, and no kit, cannot make one that verifies;** reported in `recoveries`; and the invariant it rests on: no entry holding `recovery` without `members` can verify as a signer under a group key it held as a `members` holder;
- custody seals: wrong company key version, wrong area, wrong area version, a seal naming a roster off the chain, a replayed seal for another area all fail; a re-seal by someone who is not a data admin on the roster it names fails; a seal that opens to a key other than the committed one is reported, with its sealer;
- the company seed opens only for the holder and key version named; a seed whose public keys are not the chain's is refused;
- **the kit seal's proof:** a kit seal made with junk in place of the seed fails the check and names its sealer; a proof for another kit seal fails; `sealWithCoins` with the same inputs gives the same bytes in TS and Rust (fixtures);
- the escrow and `previousEnvelope` checks: one that opens to a seed whose public keys are not the chain's is reported;
- the kit: the derivation of all four key pairs is fixed by fixtures; its signing keys verify nothing but a recovery-roster statement; a kit of another company opens nothing; the escrow opens only under the company key version it names; **an old kit opens nothing after the roster that replaced it**, given only what the server stores from that roster on;
- `previousEnvelope` chains: a data admin at version 3 opens seals of versions 1 and 2, and someone who held only version 1 opens nothing of 2;
- the recovery request: verifies only under an entry that holds `recovery`, for that kit and company key version;
- **personal keys never enter a custody package:** the sealing functions refuse a workspace the chain does not list for that company, and the worker has no call that passes a personal workspace's key to them (a test scans the worker's exports, as `server-imports.test.ts` scans imports);
- area lists: an addition that removes a person, lowers a role or lowers `everyone` is refused; one signed by a non-manager is refused; one that names someone not active on its roster is refused; a list naming a roster off the chain is refused; a served list short of the device's own record is refused; a base list whose digest is not the roster row's, or that names its own committing roster, is refused; two lists with the same people have different digests (the nonce);
- **after leaving:** an addition and a share record signed by a removed manager, naming a roster from before the removal, are refused when they lie past the heads the removal roster committed, and accepted when within them; a list built on a refused addition is refused; **after coming back:** a re-admitted person gets no standing from a list that names a roster before the one that last let them in;
- **heads:** a roster whose rows do not hash to its digest is refused; a device ahead of a committed head reports it and one behind refuses to write until it has caught up; the hidden-addition case end to end: an addition served only to the person added, their removal, and the adder's device raising it at the next roster; a new base list that lacks a hidden addition tells the addition's maker; **the furthest head wins:** a later roster whose row for an area is below one an earlier roster committed moves nothing on the device, is reported, and is a fork when its lower point is not on the same record;
- **when an override is made:** a device ahead of a roster that changed no key in that area makes none, keeps the stop in its own log and applies the ordinary wait; the same device ahead of a roster that changed that area's key, or of a family checkpoint, makes one for each stop, batch stop and lowering record the head lacks; a device that synced after the roster and read the stop from the served log behaves as the first does;
- **when an override goes:** when a committed head holds an entry at least as restrictive, and not before; when its node is purged or its folder retired, and not while the node is in Trash; never because the node's key changed;
- **settings marks:** the layouts of all four kinds are fixed by fixtures; a `granter` mark makes a second device treat every share whose latest record names that granter as stopped, and not one that was handed on; any device of the person drops a `share` or `granter` mark once its own committed head covers it, the device that wrote it being gone; a 33rd mark folds the fullest folder's marks into one `area` mark and loses none; under an `area` mark a device does not walk that folder and re-seals nothing in it; a mark is written into a full document by forgetting removals, and a pin that would eat the marks' reserve is refused; a document sealed by a client that does not know the field still carries it after that client saves;
- **forks:** a committed list or share-log head that is not on the device's record, and that nothing served reaches, warns at once and is reported, at any key version, the key change's own roster included; **the hidden stop with another entry in its place, end to end:** the stopper's device warns, posts the stop again, the subtree is owed again, and the device takes the committed log as its base **and keeps the stop as an override: it does not re-seal that share when it walks, does not write under that node, and goes on posting the stop, until a committed head holds a stop for it; another device's key change does not clear the override; a second device of the same person that reads the settings mark does not re-seal the share either; a lowered role survives the same way;** a dismissal that is unsigned, signed by someone without `members`, or names other digests leaves the Admins' note showing, and no dismissal clears an override; a device never stays behind: a withheld extension becomes a fork; a later roster whose head the record reaches clears the warning; a hidden addition replaced by another at the same list version is a fork for its maker, whose re-post is refused as stale and who is told to add the person again;
- **writer verdicts in a company folder:** an active colleague who is not on the latest list, a viewer, a former data admin and an editor whose addition was withheld all read `not-on-this-folder`, never `bad-signature`; with the reader's list behind or forked the verdict is `unchecked`; a colleague who edits by a share record reads verified by the group; **a writer the reader has personally checked, who is not on the folder, still reads `not-on-this-folder`;** a signature that fails under the roster's keys still reads `bad-signature`;
- the resolver: membership alone, a former list member, a suspended member, a share grantee outside the shared subtree, a data admin without a session, and a data admin in a key-change session asking for a download URL all get nothing;
- **share log:** a walker drops a share with no record, a record signed by a non-entitled or departed granter not handed on, a record for other grantee keys, a grantee marked left after the record, an expired one, a link whose secret does not hash to `tokenDigest`; **a stopped share whose row and record the server restores is not re-sealed by a device that saw the stop, and a log served without the stop is refused by that device; a walk by a device that never saw the stop is reported by one that did, and the subtree becomes owed again;** a record with a `seq` that repeats or skips is refused; nothing follows a stop; a role raised back by replaying an earlier record is refused; a handed-on record verifies only when the earlier granter is left; a family rotation without a checkpoint over a non-empty log is treated as behind, and a checkpoint posted afterwards clears it only when it verifies over the log the device holds; **a walker drops a share when the served policy forbids it and never keeps one because the served policy allows it;** a 21st record for one share is refused by the device before it is sent; **a batch stop ends every share and link of a left granter up to its position except those it lists, is refused for a granter who is not left, from a signer without `members`, or over a position that is not on the verified log, and nothing follows it for a share it covers; a share handed on before a batch stop was appended is not covered by it, whatever position the batch stop names;**
- writes under a stopped share's node wait until its key epoch passes the stop's;
- **browse notices:** a notice opens for a holder of the folder key and not for a member who is not on the folder; notice keys that are not the ones the folder key derives are reported, naming whoever made that key; a notice whose statement is not signed by a data admin on the roster it names reads as invalid, and the server refuses to open a session on such a statement; the previous-seed envelope opens earlier notices and yields no folder key;
- activity records: a record replayed on another action, a wrong `factDigest`, a signature under another device's key all fail; a data admin cannot produce a record that verifies as a member's; **one record wrapped in three facts counts once and blames nobody; two different records with one `seq` are flagged as the app's; a record whose clock is far from its fact's time is not corroborated for when; a fact timed well before the link before its own reads as recorded late; a fact timed ahead of the next link, or of the reader's clock, reads as timed ahead and does not make honest rows after it read late; unused numbers show nothing;** a thumbnails record verifies only over the digest of exactly the ids in its fact;
- **the sealed fact:** a row opens only with the epoch key, and the epoch key only with the company key of the version it names; a fact whose record names another action, area, node or subject than the fact reads as the server's word, not corroborated; **a row that commits after a later id was already linked falls into the next link, and every link still verifies** (the id-range defect of the second draft); a link over reordered, dropped or altered stamped rows fails.

**Server (Postgres, through the real routes where rows matter):**

- the denied-path suite: a colleague's employee area by guessed id (list, open, download URL, thumbnail, rotation work, share log, notices); a shared folder one is not on; a data admin against a personal workspace, with and without a session; a suspended member on every company and Drive route; **a join requester on routes 3, 5 and 6**;
- `deleteAccount` leaves company areas and deletes only personal workspaces;
- seats: two admissions racing for the last seat; auto-admit never buys; an operator's raised limit takes effect under the lock;
- the company roster locks only the areas it touches (a concurrent Drive write in an untouched area is not blocked) and a departure locks the leaver's; an addition signed by someone being removed cannot land after their removal commits;
- at most 8 rotations per call; `ORG_AREA_LIMIT` counted under the lock; owed key-change rosters do not spend the ordinary roster budget, and an admission is still accepted while 63 of them land;
- a removal makes exactly the leaver's areas owed, all of them for a data admin; **revokes every share granted to the leaver and makes those subtrees owed; pauses every share and link they made;** writes there answer `rotating` until each key change lands;
- the two-person rule: a held change lands only after a second data admin's approval or the wait; the filer cannot approve their own; a removal is never held; nothing is held in a company of one;
- **recovery timing:** a request to use the kit releases on an Owner's approval and not on an Admin's; an Admin's objection moves the release to 7 days from the request; any Owner cancels, and an Admin cannot; for 7 days after a recovery roster the recovered Owner's roster that removes, suspends or demotes an earlier Owner, and their new kit, are refused, **and the same from an Owner the recovered one made is refused too;** an earlier Owner's are accepted, and they remove the recovered one at once;

## 9. Where the directions and the cryptography disagree

1. **"The Owner holds `members` only by default" (plan, Roles).** Not achievable with this core. A roster row for a new or rotated folder is computed from the key itself, by the signer, and the person who lets someone in makes their folder. So every `members` holder handles folder keys, and a modified app keeps them. Offering "manages people, cannot read files" would be a claim the code does not keep. This design pairs `members` with `data_admin`. The alternative is a two-device protocol in which a folder's own people make its key and a manager signs only its commitment; it needs the folder's people online for every removal, which is the wrong trade for offboarding.
2. **"The recovery kit removes the single-admin lockout" (plan, Backup custodian).** Only with the recovery roster (2.1), the kit's own signing keys on the roster, and the group key's escrow (2.2). The plan's kit held the company key alone, which opens files but can never sign a roster, and nobody would be entitled to fetch what it opens. And no kit helps a company of one whose only person loses their account: the kit needs a member holding `recovery` to use it. For a company of one, the person's own recovery phrase is the backup.
3. **The kit is a second company key.** The plan treats it as a backup. It is a key that opens everything and that only a new company key retires: whoever made it or kept it knows it, and the server stores the current company key sealed to it. So a kit, with a copy of the server's database, reads every company file with no trace on any roster. The "needs an account holding `recovery`" and every wait around using it are server rules. This design ties the kit to the company key on the chain (2.1, rule 7), makes the kit's own signature the thing a recovery roster needs (rule 11), lets only an Owner replace it (rule 8), and says the rest plainly: keep the kit like the key it is.
4. **"An admin can overwrite device approval one time" against "no admin ever approves a member's device".** They are compatible only if the company's requirement gates the company's space and not the account (3.9). An account-wide gate that an admin could waive would let an admin lower the protection on a person's personal space. This changes `devices-design.md` 4, which had the policy switch the level on for the account.
5. **"Only if they have registered a device first."** Read here as: the first device a member ever uses for the company is never blocked, and a waiver needs such a device on record. The first draft also let someone with no device signed in start again as if new, which let a stolen password in whenever the member happened to be signed out. That reading is gone: once a member has had a device, every later one is approved, waived, or refused.
6. **Auto-join against first use.** First use already lets the server plant a newcomer's keys when an Admin lets them in without a check. Auto-join removes the Admin's click as well, so the server alone, or anyone with a mailbox at the domain, gets a member admitted. It is confined to Member, to folders open to everyone, to paid seats, to a fresh mailbox proof, to people who were never members, and to a daily count that is on the chain. It cannot be made safe against the server; it is stated instead.
7. **"The audit records download, copy, delete, kept offline, shared."** The server witnesses downloads, copies inside the company, deletes and shares, and seals what it witnessed. "Kept offline", "copied out", "printed" and the reason for any download are the app's word. A thumbnail read and a full download are the same request to the storage, so both are listed. And the server's own fact is the server's word: only a record signed by the person's device corroborates it (2.5). The ledger shows which is which. It must not be sold as proof of what someone did with a file.
8. **"Everything needs to be end-to-end encrypted."** The contents, names and details already were. The server's own history of requests now is too. Four things cannot be: who is in the company and on each folder's list, which the server must read to refuse everyone else; the share log, which it must read to serve a share; a rule's count while its window is open; and each request at the moment it is served. A fifth is not new and is not sealed either: Drive's own columns for who last wrote which item id and when, in every space. Section 5 lists all of it, and what this design stops writing because it was read history. Evaluating alerts on admins' devices instead would remove the counters and the readable rule conditions, at the price of alerts that fire only while an admin's app is running; the owner was shown that trade and it was not taken.
9. **"Each admin session recorded and visible to the member."** With no readable history on the server, the member can only be told by something sealed to keys the folder's people hold. The admin's device signs a statement that it is opening the folder; the server checks the signature, opens no session without it, and seals the statement and its own clock to the folder's notice keys (3.7). So "who" is the admin's signature and "when" is the server's clock. That every session has a notice is the server's rule. The admins' ledger has the server's sealed fact as well. An admin who is one of a folder's people is not browsing, and writes no notice.
10. **"A detail record signed by the member."** Signed, but with an Ed25519 key the member's hybrid signature certified, not with a hybrid signature per record. That matches file records today and is not post-quantum for forgery of a record. It should move when writer signatures do.
11. **Removing a data admin.** "Rotation when an admin is removed" in the plan reads as replacing one key. It is that, plus a key change for every folder in the company, because the old key opened them all. At 500 folders that is hours of background work across Admins' devices, with every folder read-only until its turn. Companies should have few data admins, and the screen must say what removing one costs.
12. **"A stopped share stays stopped."** True in front of any device that saw the stop. Against a server that hides the stop from the device doing a key change, it is noticed afterwards, not prevented (2.4). The design that prevents it closes every client link at each key change until its granter is next online.
13. **50 seats on the family limits.** The shipped server caps a group at 50 folders and locks all of them on every roster with changes. 3.11 replaces both for companies. It is real work in the repository's most delicate transaction and belongs in PR 4's review.

**Where this revision does not follow the review's recommendation, and why.**

- **Finding 3, the kit.** The review asked that dropping `data_admin` from anyone force a new kit. A plain Admin never held the kit's secret: only a `recovery` holder is meant to have it, and a kit's maker is an Owner, who holds `recovery`. So the rule is on `recovery` alone. Removing an Admin already replaces the company key; it does not also make someone write down 24 new words.
- **Finding 8, the recovered role.** The review noted the result was not an Owner and left the fix open. The recovery roster now makes an Owner, because with the Owner rule nothing less can remove the lost one.
- **Finding 12, the chain.** The review recommended a chain per folder under the row lock Drive already takes. Downloads take no such lock, and a thousand-file download would queue behind every write. The chain is per company, built by the worker once a minute over ciphertext, with no lock in any request.
- **Finding 12, the sequence.** The review recommended a contiguous `seq` with a small window, checked by the server. A server-side sequence per activity key is a readable count of what each device did. The server tracks none; the admin's device finds repeats and gaps.
- **Finding 7, thumbnails.** One fact per thumbnails call naming every version id, not one row per id: the same information in a hundredth of the rows.
- **Finding 1, where the head is committed.** The review asked for the share log's head on the roster row at each area rotation. For a company it is in `heads`, on every roster, which covers rotations and also catches a hidden stop between them. A family's format 2 roster has no such field, so a family rotation carries a signed checkpoint instead.

**Where the third revision does not follow the second review's recommendation, and why.**

- **Finding 3, write times.** The review recommended stopping or coarsening the read traces and coarsening node timestamps to the day. The two read traces are stopped. Write times are left as they are. Coarsening them would touch every Drive write in a company's areas, in the repository's largest module, and the claim would still have to say "who wrote which item, on which day", because the signer ids stay. The change that removes the trace is the review's third option, signer ids and times inside the sealed metadata, which is a change to Drive in every space and is the owner's to decide (10.38). `use_count` is kept, as a count of visits by people with no account that the link's maker is shown.
- **Finding 12, who browsed.** Adopted, and made stronger. A note sealed by the server alone would be the server's word for who. Here the admin signs the statement, the server refuses a session without a signature that verifies, and the folder's people verify it again. The second draft's note written by the admin's app is gone.
- **Finding 4, forks.** Adopted, both parts, plus the rule that ends one: after it has warned and posted what was missing, the device takes the committed record as its base. Without that, re-posting cannot end a fork, because the server has one log and the device's own no longer matches it at any index. (For chaining only, since the fourth revision: what took access away is kept, 2.4.)
- **Finding 10, the undo link.** The review recommended it on every primary change. It is sent only when the change was made without a second step. With the step passed, the attack it answers (a stolen password alone) cannot have happened, and an undo link sent anyway would hand a leaver's former employer a week's veto over that person moving off a work mailbox.
- **Finding 2, the hold.** Adopted, and extended to a new kit: a recovered Owner could otherwise replace the kit in their first minute and leave the returning Owner without one.
- **Finding 7, the wedge.** Adopted, and extended to "Start over with new keys" for the only Owner, which wedges a company the same way leaving does. (Taken back in the fourth revision: nobody is refused "Start over". And the refusal to leave became a notice, for a kit holder in the fourth and for every role in the fifth.)
- **Finding 6, gaps.** A missing number blames nobody. The second draft read a gap as "a record is missing"; a request that fails uses a number up, so honest devices leave gaps every day.
- **Finding 8, budgets.** The review asked for a per-share and a per-member budget and gave no numbers. 20 and 100 a day were this document's. (The fourth revision replaced the daily figure with a cap of 500 live shares and a 1,000-a-day backstop, 2.4.)

**What the fourth revision takes from the third review, and where it goes further.**

- **Finding 3, Owners shut out.** The review recommended stating it and warning, and left "only an Owner makes keys" to the owner. Both are taken (2.1, rule 12; 2.2, "A holder without a seal"). It is the right rule: rule 8 made the Owner rank a fact of the chain for entries, and a rank that any Admin could empty by making the next key was not one. What it forces, beyond what the review listed:
    - **an Admin cannot leave on their own roster,** and no other Admin can mark them left. Their leave is a notice, and after 7 days it takes effect whatever the Owners did (3.6);
    - the fourth revision also froze every roster once an Admin's departure was recorded, until an Owner marked it. The fifth took that back (below): it protected nothing the notice week had not, and any Admin could have set it off;
    - **an Admin's unsigned identity change** (starting over) is an Owner's to put right, as is a chosen replacement of the company key;
    - two Admins who fall out can only pause each other. An Owner settles it.
- **Finding 1, forgetting a stop.** Adopted as recommended, with one change to the directions this revision was given. An override was to go when "the committed log contains it or the node's key epoch has passed it". The second half is wrong: a key change made by a device that never saw the stop re-seals the share under the new key, and that is exactly when the override is the last thing that knows. It goes only when the committed record has the stop (2.4, "Overrides").
- **Finding 4, trapped accounts.** Adopted for a kit holder and for "Start over". The fourth revision kept an Owner's own leave refused "until they step down", as if that were in their own hands. It is not (below).
- **Finding 5, budgets.** Both fixes, and the daily budget stays as a wide backstop (1,000) instead of going: a cap on live shares does not stop an app that makes and stops one share in a loop, and every signer has to verify each of those entries.
- **Finding 7, an exposed Owner.** The fresh check before another Owner folds in is taken. It does not close the first half of the finding: until someone removes that Owner, whoever holds their keys is that Owner. Nothing in a design where a key is the person closes that.
- **Finding 8, reports.** Kept allowed in company areas, recorded and alertable, not made a policy. A company that could turn reporting off for its own files could hide abuse in them.

**What the fifth revision takes from the fourth review.** That round found no break; its findings were flows that could not finish or that stalled. All five are applied as recommended.

- **Finding 1, an Owner could not step down.** Rule 12 made it impossible without anyone noticing: stepping down to Member drops `members`, the succession that follows must be signed by an Owner on that roster, and the person stepping down no longer is one. So an Owner whose fellow Owner did nothing could never have left. Owners now give notice like Admins and kit holders (3.6). Only the last Owner is refused, and what they must do first, making someone else an Owner, is a roster they can sign.
- **Finding 3, the freeze.** A departure that only an Owner can mark no longer stops other rosters. The price is stated where it falls (3.6): the departed person's keys stay the current ones until an Owner acts. That was already true for the 7 days before.
- **Finding 2, overrides.** All four fixes. The one that changes what devices do is when an override is made: only when a key change, or a fork, passed over a stop. The fourth revision's rule turned an everyday race on an honest server into a folder that behaved differently on different devices for as long as no roster happened to be signed.
- **Finding 4, the settings mark.** Specified in full (2.4). One addition: the 33rd mark folds into a coarser one instead of pushing the oldest out, because a dropped mark is a stop the person's other devices forget.
- **Finding 5, account deletion without notice.** The review left it open. A deletion by someone who must give notice becomes a pending deletion that completes when the notice runs out. Refusing it until the person came back a week later would have been simpler to build and worse to live with.
- **Finding 5, two claims.** Both are corrected to what holds: the kit does not help an Owner whom another Owner and the server left out, and an Admin, with the server, can write keys into another Admin's entry. The second touches what a browse notice proves (3.7). The rule that would close it is in the open list, not in PR 1: a chain rule added in the last revision before a build is one no review round has attacked.

## 10. Decisions

**Taken on 10 November 2026, unless the owner objects.** The owner was shown these six and did not object:

1. **No role manages people without reading files.** Admin is `members` with `data_admin`; Billing is the role that never holds the company key; the Owner holds all four. This reverses "the Owner holds `members` only by default" (9.1).
2. **A company's device approval gates the company's space, not the account** (9.4). The account-wide switch stays the person's own choice.
3. **A second-step code does not approve a device.** Another of the person's devices, the recovery phrase, or a waiver does. No 72-hour email wait.
4. **Only Admins create top-level shared folders** in this release; members share from their own area.
5. **Removing a data admin changes every folder's key,** with the cost on the screen and a prompt to keep data admins few.
6. **After a removal, the folders the person could open are read-only until their key change** (3.6 says exactly what that protects).

**Taken on the recommendation,** as the owner agreed for the smaller ones, each as first written unless marked:

7. The recovery roster and the group key's escrow are built, with a dedicated review of the recovery roster rule. **Changed in the third revision:** a recovery roster also needs the kit's own signature (decision 19).
8. Auto-join's limits: Member only, paid seats only, 10 a day by default, folders open to everyone only, marked until reviewed. **Changed:** also a fresh mailbox proof, never a former member or a declined account, and the day and count signed on the roster. **Third revision:** a leaver's addresses are barred too (decision 41).
9. Extra emails never sign in and never receive recovery or security mail.
10. **What a leaver shared. Reversed:** paused at the removal, and stopped unless an Admin keeps it. The first draft kept them by default; a leaver's share to an address of their own is the simplest way to keep reading.

Changed by the owner's direction:** the server keeps no ledger it can read and no read history; every thumbnail read is listed, kept three months, everything else twelve. **Third revision:** a thumbnails call carries the member's record too; an activity key's row keeps a month and no device id; Drive's own write columns are counted, not denied (decisions 38 and 39).

**From the second revision. To confirm; each has a recommendation.** Marked where the third revision changed one.

19. **Owners rank above Admins on the chain. Widened in the third revision:** only an Owner changes anything in an Owner's or a kit holder's entry, their keys included, gives or takes `recovery`, or replaces the kit; and a recovery roster needs the kit's own signature. _Recommendation: yes._ As first written it covered roles only, and an Admin could have written their own keys into an Owner's entry, or used the kit path with no kit.
20. **A new kit costs a new company key and every folder's key change,** and so does taking "can use the kit" from anyone. _Recommendation: yes, said on the screen._ The alternative is a kit that keeps opening the company after its holder has left.
21. **Rosters that change no person say `same` instead of repeating the member list.** _Recommendation: yes, reviewed with PR 1._ It is the difference between a new device downloading a few megabytes of history and over a hundred.
22. **A stop hidden by the server is noticed, not prevented.** _Recommendation: accept, and say so on the security page._ Prevention means client links close at every key change until the person who made them is next online. **Third revision:** it is noticed at once also when the server puts another entry where the stop was (2.3, "Forked").
23. **Members learn of an admin's session from a sealed notice. Changed in the third revision:** the admin's device signs the statement, the server checks it and seals it to keys the folder's people derive from the folder key. Members see who, by the admin's signature, and when, by the server's clock; not counts. _Recommendation: yes._ The second revision's note was written by the admin's own app, which a modified app could fill with junk.
24. **The server holds a ten-minute key while it writes.** For up to ten minutes a running server can read what it just sealed, and its memory may hold more, since nothing wipes it. _Recommendation: accept._ A key per row would make every thumbnail read cost a post-quantum encapsulation.
25. **No two-person hold in a company of one.** _Recommendation: yes._
26. **Counters live at most 24 hours, and an alert's window is at most 24 hours.** _Recommendation: yes._ A weekly count would need a weekly readable tally. A per-person rule is a one-day tally per person while it runs, and its screen says so.

28. **Changing an account's primary address is built here,** with extra emails. _Recommendation: yes._ **Third revision:** security mail goes to both addresses for 30 days, and a change made without a second step can be undone from the old address for 7 days (decision 40).
29. **A recovered person becomes an Owner,** not an Admin. _Recommendation: yes_ (9, first review, finding 8).
30. **Colleagues see the address a person joined with,** never another address on their account. _Recommendation: yes._
31. **Shares to someone who leaves are revoked at the removal,** by whoever made them. _Recommendation: yes, as in families._ A colleague shares again, to an outsider, if they mean to.

**New in the third revision. To confirm; each has a recommendation.** Ranked by how much each changes the build.

32. **In a family, only the person who shared, or a manager, stops a share; and writes under that item wait until a manager's device has changed the folder's keys.** _Recommendation: yes._ The second revision let any member stop any share. In a family a stop makes the whole folder's key change due, and only managers can do it, so any member could have frozen writes in any shared subtree until a manager was next online. This changes behaviour for families, which is why it is here.
33. **Using the kit: only an Owner's approval shortens the 48 hours; any Admin's objection makes it 7 days; only an Owner cancels; and for 7 days a recovered Owner cannot remove or demote an earlier Owner or replace the kit.** _Recommendation: yes._ Admins cannot cancel, on purpose: if they could, one hostile Admin could block a genuine recovery for ever. They can slow it and call the Owners. Every one of these is a server rule; against the server nothing waits. **Fourth revision:** the 7-day hold covers anyone's roster that touches an earlier Owner or the kit, not only the recovered person's (decision 45).
34. **Leaving as an Owner. Changed in the fifth revision:** an Owner gives 7 days' notice, like an Admin, and is out after 7 days whatever anyone does; only the last Owner is refused, until they have made someone else an Owner (decision 46). _Recommendation: yes._ As first written, an Owner had to "step down" before leaving. Since decision 44 that is a roster an Owner cannot sign for themselves, so an Owner whose fellow Owner did nothing could never have left or deleted their account. A last Owner who is the only person left closes the company by leaving, as a family's last member does; one with colleagues and no successor removes them first.
35. **A data admin who starts over with new keys makes the company replace its key and every folder's.** _Recommendation: accept, and say so on the "Start over" screen for anyone who is an Admin._ The keys that opened their seal are no longer vouched for; nothing lighter is sound. **Fourth revision:** it is an Owner who puts it right (decision 44), and nobody is refused "Start over", the only Owner included (decision 46).
36. **Admins' devices read who is on every folder, and what is shared from each, to sign rosters, and those reads are not in the ledger.** _Recommendation: accept, and say it in the disclosure every folder shows._ They hold the company key anyway, and a fact for every roster an Admin signs would bury the log in noise. They read ids and accounts, not names or contents.
37. **Download URLs in a company's areas live 15 minutes, not an hour.** _Recommendation: yes._ It bounds how long a removed person keeps fetching. A long download asks for a new URL more often, and each is a new line in the log.
38. **Drive's own columns for who last wrote which item, and when, stay readable for now.** They are in every space today (section 5). _Recommendation: move the signer ids and times inside the sealed metadata as a later change to Drive in every space, designed with the writer-signature upgrade (`identity-pq-design.md` 7), and until then say plainly on the security page what the tables show._ Its cost: every client and the Rust core verify a record from sealed fields; the server can no longer check a record's signature when it is written, nor clear a deleted account's ids from its files; retention still needs a coarse date on the server; report snapshots change. Not recommended: coarsening write times to the day in company areas only, which touches every Drive write for a claim that would still read "who, and on which day".
39. **In a company's areas the server stops writing when an object was last read and when a link was last opened, and keeps counting a link's visits.** _Recommendation: yes._ The cost is cold-storage tiering by age alone there (no cold class is set at hushos.com today). The alternative for the count is to drop it too, and then a link's maker no longer sees "Opened 3 times" unless they are an Admin.
40. **A primary address change made without a second step can be undone from the old address for 7 days.** _Recommendation: yes._ It stops a stolen password moving an account's security mail. The cost: a leaver with no second step can have their move off a work address undone by whoever reads that mailbox, for a week. The screen offers the second step first.
41. **A removed or declined person's addresses are barred from automatic joining until an Admin clears them.** _Recommendation: yes._ Otherwise a leaver whose mailbox still works moves the address to a new account and is let back in by auto-join. It is a server rule.
42. **Device approval rows are kept from the first day, policy or not, and a waiver is for one named request.** _Recommendation: yes._ Otherwise turning the policy on makes the next device to arrive a "first" one, and a waiver goes to whoever gets there first.
43. **Share log limits. Changed in the fourth revision:** 20 records for one share or link, 500 live shares and links of one member's making, and 1,000 entries a member a day as a backstop; stops by an Admin or a walker, batch stops and hand-overs count against none (decision 47). _Recommendation: yes, all operator settings._ Every signer checks every new entry before a roster.

**New in the fourth revision. To confirm; each has a recommendation.** Ranked by how much each changes the build.

44. **Only an Owner removes or demotes an Admin. Taken on 10 November 2026 on recommendation; the owner to confirm.** Only an Owner signs a new group key or a new company key, and removing an Admin makes both. An Admin still lets people in, removes Members, makes another Admin and manages folders. _Recommendation: yes._ Without it an Admin, with the server's help, can leave every Owner out of the next keys for good. The cost: a company whose only Owner is away cannot remove an Admin until they are back (another Admin can pause them), and an Admin's own leave goes through a notice (decision 46).
45. **After the kit is used, nothing touches an earlier Owner or the kit for 7 days unless an earlier Owner signs it.** _Recommendation: yes._ Holding only the recovered person's own rosters was not enough: they could make a second Owner who was not held. A server rule.
46. **Leaving with a role. Changed in the fifth revision.** An Owner, an Admin, and someone who only "can use the kit" give 7 days' notice and are paused at once; after 7 days they are out, whatever anyone did. The last Owner makes someone else an Owner first. "Start over with new keys" warns and is never refused. **And a company that has not acted by then keeps working:** other Admins go on letting people in and managing folders. _Recommendation: yes._ The cost falls on a company whose Owners do not act: until an Owner finishes the removal, the keys that person held stay in use and the old kit stays good, as they were through the notice week, and the company's page says so every day. The fourth revision instead stopped every change to people, folders and keys from day 8, which any Admin could have set off by giving notice while the only Owner was away.
47. **One signed entry stops everything a leaver shared in a folder, except what an Admin keeps; and a member has at most 500 live shares and links.** _Recommendation: yes._ It is what lets an Admin answer for a few hundred shares in a minute, not over days.
48. **Anyone can still report a company file to the people who run the service, which hands them that file's key.** It is recorded in the company's activity when filed and each time an operator opens it, the report screen says what it does, and an alert can match it. _Recommendation: yes, and no policy to turn it off._ A company able to switch reporting off for its own files could hide abuse in them. On hushos.com "the people who run the service" is HushOS; self-hosted, it is whoever runs that server.

**From the review of PR 1. For the owner to decide; no recommendation is built in.**

56. **Who may make someone an Admin.** Rule 8 leaves "plant, then promote" open across two rosters (2.1): an Admin with the server's help writes keys of their own into a Member's entry, then promotes that entry, and holds an Admin's keys under a colleague's name. Two ways to settle it:
    - **(a) Only an Owner makes someone an Admin.** One more clause in rule 8: a roster that gives `members` to anyone is signed by an Owner. It closes this on the chain, for every device, with no reliance on the signer's own device, and it makes "who can create a signer" the same short list as "who can replace a key". Its cost: it reverses "an Admin may make another Admin under the two-person rule" (2.2), so a company whose only Owner is away cannot add an Admin until they are back; the two-person rule for `add-data-admin` then always has an Owner as its filer; it is a chain rule, so it changes `checkRosterOrg` in both twins, the fixtures, and what PR 5's role screens offer an Admin; and it needs its own review round.
    - **(b) Keep it and state the residual.** Nothing changes in the build. An Admin with the server's help can do this; it is visible to every device as a promotion with its signer's name, the real Member's device warns about its own entry, and it gives the planter nothing an Admin could not get by promoting an account of their own. Its cost: the roster can name a colleague as an Admin who is not one, until someone reads the warning or the promotion and an Owner removes the entry and replaces the company key.
    - As built: (b). Moving to (a) after launch tightens the rule for new rosters only and needs no format change.

**Open, with what each costs.** Nothing here blocks the build; each is a known limit.

- Every member sees every folder's id, when it was made or re-keyed, and how often its list and share log change (2.3). Hiding it needs per-folder commitments off the roster.
- Outside editors rely on the server to refuse writes under a key that is owed a change (2.4).
- A new device's first check of a long history is tens of seconds; past a minute it needs a checkpoint that is not designed (3.11).
- The share log is never compacted. A new device hashes all of it: a few megabytes at ten thousand entries.
- An old identity key of a data admin still opens the old seal's bytes if someone kept them, after a signed replacement (2.2). Replacing the company key is the remedy, by choice.
- A hostile server can write a false fact into the ledger (2.5). Only a device's signed record corroborates one.
- Inside the current minute the ledger's chain protects nothing: the server can leave out or reorder rows no link covers yet (2.5).
- A hostile server can open an admin's session and seal no notice (3.7). It cannot name an admin who did not sign.
- Every wait around the recovery kit is a server rule (2.2). The chain says only that the kit was needed and that it was used.
- A company of one whose only person starts over with new keys can no longer sign for it: their entry names keys they no longer hold, and the recovery roster needs those keys too. The kit still opens the files, so the screen tells them to copy the files out and make the company again.
- A point-in-time backup of the database holds the time of every sealed row's insert (2.5).
- After a fork is settled, the device has taken the server's record on the server's word for everything it had not itself verified, except what took access away, which it keeps as overrides (2.4). The Admins' note stays until one of them signs its dismissal.
- A device that never saw a stop, and none of whose person's other devices did, can still be walked into re-sealing it (2.4). Devices that saw it say so until the record has it.
- One Owner with the server's help can leave another Owner out of the next keys (2.2). The one left out is warned at once and every member's device shows who replaced the keys. The kit is no remedy: the same Owner would seal junk to it, and the one left out cannot check.
- An Admin with the server's help can write keys of their own into a **Member's** entry on one roster and make that Member an Admin on the next (2.1). The server's identity check stops the first step and a fresh words check stops the second, and the second is a device rule that the planting Admin's own device ignores. Every device sees the promotion and who signed it, and the real Member's device warns that its own entry names keys it does not hold. It is equal in power to "an Admin may make another Admin"; what it adds is a colleague's name on keys that are not theirs. An Admin's and an Owner's entries are the chain's to guard (rule 8). Decision 56 is whether to close it.
- Whoever holds an Owner's keys and a copy of their seals is that Owner until another Owner removes them (2.2).
- Someone who left with a role still holds the company's current keys, and their entry still reads as that role, until an Owner's roster marks them (3.6). An honest server serves them nothing; a server working with them could still pass what they sign to members' devices. The same holds for the kit of a kit holder who has left.
- A stop stored in the same instant as a roster that does not touch its folder can miss that roster's heads (2.3). Devices that saw it are ahead until the next roster, which is routine and costs nothing.
- A coarse settings mark (`area`, `group`) can be lifted only by the device that wrote it, or by the person by hand (2.4).
- A leaver whose company mail still reaches them at another address can be let back in by auto-join (3.2).
