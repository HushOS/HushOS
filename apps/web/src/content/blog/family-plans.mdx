HushOS has family plans from today. Up to 20 people share one plan's storage, and it covers both their own files and the family folders they share, end-to-end encrypted. Everyone's own files stay private to them. Families are on the web for now; the iPhone and Android apps follow.

## What a family is

A family is a group of HushOS accounts with folders they share. Every member can open the family folders, and nobody else can, us included. Their keys are made and sealed on members' own devices, the same way your own files are.

Each member keeps their own files. My files stays private from the rest of the family, exactly as it is private from us. The family folders sit beside it, under the family's name. An account can be in one family at a time.

One person organizes the family. They can let others manage it too: invite people, let them in, make family folders and remove someone.

## One plan's storage

A family's storage covers everyone's own files and the family folders together. There is no separate allowance to keep track of: a file in My files and a file in a family folder draw from the same plan.

Moving files between My files and a family folder uses no extra storage, because both sides are the same plan. Your device does the move: it downloads each file, locks it again under the keys of where it is going, uploads it, and the original is deleted as the copy lands. Only the current version moves, and the move dialog says what else is lost before it starts.

The people who manage the family see how much space each person's own files take, rounded up to the whole gigabyte. They never see the files' names, how many there are, or what is in them. Everyone in the family sees how much the family stores in all, counted the same way, so nobody can work out exactly how much someone else stores.

On a phone, your own files work as before, and the storage the app shows is the family's.

## If you already pay for a plan

When you are let into a family that has a plan, your own plan ends at that moment and the days you paid for but won't use are refunded, prorated, through Polar. The join page tells you this before you accept.

While your files use the family's storage, you can't buy a plan of your own. If you leave later, the plan that ended doesn't come back: you are on Free until you choose one.

The same goes for whoever starts the family: their own plan ends, with the unused days refunded, when the family's plan starts.

## Joining

You invite someone by email, and the invitation is open for seven days. They sign in, or make an account, and accept. Someone already in another family has to leave it first. Then the two of you check each other, in person or on a call. Not in a message sent through HushOS.

First, the newcomer reads the twelve words on their join screen and the person letting them in compares them. That is the same check as when you share with someone: it proves the keys HushOS shows for them are really theirs.

Then both screens show a join code. The person letting them in reads all twelve words. The newcomer's screen hides two of them, so the newcomer compares the other ten and types the two they could not see.

**Why the join code.** A new member's device has to learn who is in the family and which keys are whose. It learns that from a signed member list, which the server stores and hands out. A dishonest server could hand a newcomer a different list, with an extra member whose keys it holds, and the newcomer's device would lock family files for them too. The join code is worked out from the member list each screen was given, so matching words mean both of you see the same list. The newcomer's device then pins those first keys, and every later version of the list has to follow on from them, signed by someone who manages the family.

The two hidden words make sure the check really happens. Ten words can be skimmed and agreed to without listening. Two words that only the other person's screen shows cannot be typed without hearing them. The newcomer's device picks which two at random and never tells the server.

If the words do not match, the newcomer's device keeps nothing, and HushOS says not to use the family until the two of you have sorted it out.

The join code protects a family from the server. It does not protect a family from the people who manage it: they decide who is in it.

A newcomer's own files join the family's storage when they are let in, so they have to fit in the family's free space. If they don't, the person letting them in is asked to free up space or move to a bigger plan first.

## When someone leaves

Someone who manages the family chooses Remove from family. The person removed loses the family folders straight away, and the device of whoever removed them replaces the keys of every family folder, which can take a few minutes. Someone who leaves on their own loses the folders straight away too, and their keys are replaced the next time a manager opens the family. Either way, their own files stay theirs.

Their own files go back to their own storage: Free, unless they choose a plan. If the files take more than that, they can still open, download and delete everything, but can't upload until they make room. Nothing is deleted.

Once their keys are replaced, nothing they kept opens anything new. Until then (for someone who leaves on their own, until a manager next opens the family), files added to the family folders are still locked under keys they had. Files from before they left stay sealed under the keys they had, so anything they could open then stays readable to them if they ever got a copy of the server's data.

Files keep their authors through all of that. Every file in a family folder carries a signature from whoever wrote it. Replacing keys means sealing every file's keys again, and the easy way would sign each file as the work of whoever replaced the keys. HushOS keeps the writer's original signature instead, and the person replacing the keys signs only that they sealed it again. Info on the file shows both: Written by Sam, Keys replaced by Maya. Your device opens the original and the copy sealed again and compares them itself, so nobody can attach Sam's signature to something Sam did not write.

A file written by someone who has since been removed reads "Written by Sam, who has left Lindqvist family." That mark is on purpose. Someone who has left may still hold the keys they had, and a signature carries no date anyone can trust, so HushOS cannot tell an old file of theirs from one made to look old. It says what it knows instead of vouching for them.

## When a plan or a family ends

Whoever pays can cancel the plan from the family's page. It stays until the end of the time paid for. When it ends, everyone's own files go back to their own storage, as when someone leaves, and until someone chooses a plan again nobody can upload to the family folders or be let in. Nothing is deleted.

A family ends when its last member leaves it or deletes their account. If its plan is still renewing, whoever pays cancels it first; it then runs to the end of the time paid for, with no refund, and HushOS keeps it set to end. 30 days after a family ends, its folders and everything in them are deleted from our servers, and then, once its plan has ended, its member lists, key changes, memberships and invitations. The family's billing records are kept for accounting, and the [privacy policy](/privacy) has the exceptions.

Whoever chooses the plan pays for it. Letting in a member past the places paid for adds a place, charged straight away for the rest of the period, and only the person who pays can do that. Fewer places, or a smaller plan, start at the next renewal, and a smaller plan can be chosen only while everything the family stores fits in it.

Polar, which takes the payment, is told it is a HushOS family, who pays and how many places are paid for. It is not told the family's name or who is in it.

If you run HushOS on your own server, families are in the same code and on by default. Without billing there is no shared plan: each family folder has the space you set, and members' own files keep their own.

## What the server knows

To let the right people in, HushOS's server knows the family's name, who is in it and what each person can do, who invited and let in whom, the email addresses invitations went to, and how many family folders there are, with their sizes padded as every file's is. It already knew how much each person stores; what is new is that the people who manage a family see that figure, rounded up to the whole gigabyte. It cannot open family folders: not a file name, not a byte of what is inside.

The family's name is not encrypted because it appears in invitation emails. If you would rather it said nothing, choose a neutral one.

The [security page](/security) lists what the server learns about everything else, and the [privacy policy](/privacy) says how long each of these is kept.

## What is not done yet

- **Families in the phone apps.** Family folders aren't in the released apps yet. Until they are, you create a family, join one and use the family folders in a browser.
- **Sharing from a family folder.** You cannot share a file or make a link from inside a family folder yet. When keys are replaced, HushOS has to tell which shares were made by someone allowed to make them, and that needs shares signed at family level, which we have not built.
- **Limits for each person.** One member's own files can fill the family's storage for everyone, and then uploads stop for the whole family until there is room. A manager can see whose files take the space, but can't set a limit yet.
- **Handing over the bill.** While a plan is running, whoever pays for it can't hand it to another member, so they can't leave the family without cancelling it.
- **Large moves.** A move between My files and a family folder goes down to your device and back up, and a file over 256 MB can't be moved that way yet: download it, then upload it where it should go.
- **What someone already has.** A member who is removed keeps anything they already downloaded or kept on a device. New keys protect what is written after the keys are replaced; they cannot reach into copies someone already made.
