What you share with someone in HushOS is built to stay shut to a future quantum computer. Every share between accounts is sealed with two key exchanges at once, one of them designed to resist a quantum computer, so a copy of our database taken today stays shut even if the older method is broken years from now. You do nothing to get this: both keys are made on your device at sign-up and used for every share.

## Where a quantum computer would aim

Every file in HushOS is encrypted with a symmetric cipher, and symmetric ciphers with 256-bit keys are not what a quantum computer breaks. The place a quantum computer could hurt a service like this is sharing: when you share a folder with someone, your device seals that folder's key to their public key. If that seal rested on X25519 alone, a copy of the database taken today, plus a large enough quantum computer some years from now, would open every share ever made.

So it does not rest on X25519 alone. Your device makes a fresh X25519 key for the share and agrees a secret with the other person's identity key, and it encapsulates a second secret to the ML-KEM-768 key every identity carries. The two secrets are combined into one key, so the envelope opens only for someone who holds both private halves, and it stays shut unless both problems fall.

## Why two, and not just the new one

ML-KEM is young. X25519 has two decades of attention behind it. Sealing to both means a flaw found in either one leaves the share shut, and the cost is a larger envelope that nobody ever sees.

## Signed twice as well

A seal says who can open a share. A signature says who made it. Your identity signs every share with a person with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 together, and the other person's device checks both halves against the keys it pinned for you before it opens anything. A quantum computer that could forge Ed25519 still could not pass a share off as yours.

## The words you compare cover every key

The server hands out public keys, so HushOS lets you check them. Your identity signs a statement over all four of its public keys (X25519, ML-KEM-768, Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65) with both signing keys. The server refuses an identity whose statement does not verify, and every device checks it again.

The twelve words two people compare are worked out from all four keys, so one comparison vouches for the post-quantum keys too. From then on, a key for that person that their own keys did not sign is never used silently: sharing with them pauses until you continue or check them again. A share is never sealed to X25519 alone, so withholding someone's post-quantum key gets a server nothing.

The same holds when you stop sharing with someone. Your device re-keys the folder and re-seals it to whoever remains, and it takes their keys through your pins, not from the server directly.

The full construction, and what a quantum computer would mean for the rest of HushOS, is in [how HushOS protects your files](/security). `docs/identity-pq-design.md` in the repository fixes the byte layout.
