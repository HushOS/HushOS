Turn on two-step sign-in and your password alone no longer signs anyone in to your HushOS account. After the password, HushOS asks for one more thing that only you have: a passkey, a code from an authenticator app, or a yes from a device you already use.

Turn it on under **Account → Two-step sign-in**. All three ways work on the web today. In the iPhone and Android apps, authenticator codes work from version 1.6 and, on hushos.com, passkeys from 1.7. Approving on another device arrives in the apps with version 1.8.

## Three ways to confirm it's you

- **A passkey or security key.** Your fingerprint, your face, your device's PIN or a hardware key. Passkeys saved in a password manager and synced between your devices count, and you can add up to ten. A passkey answers only to the real HushOS, so a fake sign-in page gets nothing from it.
- **An authenticator app.** Any app that makes six-digit codes, such as the one in your password manager or on your phone. Scan a QR code once, or on a phone open your authenticator app from HushOS, and from then on the app shows a new code every 30 seconds. Each code works once.
- **Another device.** A phone or computer of yours that's already signed in with two-step sign-in confirms the new sign-in. Nothing to install and nothing to carry.

The first one you turn on switches the step on and gives you ten backup codes.

## You choose which is asked first

One way is your default, and a sign-in opens on it whenever it can be used there. It starts as the first way you turned on, and **Make default** in Account moves it. The others are one press away under **Use another way**, which changes the way for that sign-in only.

Type the code from the new screen, press **Approve**, and the new device is signed in. The phone apps, from version 1.8, can scan the new screen instead.

The code ties the approval to the screen in front of you: a request goes through only when the code matches the one that screen is showing. If you didn't ask, **This wasn't me** ends the request and sends you a notice at once.

## If you lose a device

Two things get you back in, whichever ways you use:

- **Ten backup codes.** Each works once. You can make a new set at any time, and the old set stops working.
- **Your recovery phrase.** The 24 words you saved when you made your account pass the step too.

Keep both apart from your password. No support tool, admin page or email link turns your second step off.

## Asked when it matters

HushOS asks for the step whenever a browser or phone gets a new session: signing in on a new device, or on one that was signed out. A device that stays signed in is asked at most once every 30 days, so unlocking HushOS on your own laptop doesn't ask every time.

It is also asked before the changes someone with your password would want most:

- changing your password, making a new recovery phrase or replacing your account key;
- turning the step off, or adding or removing a way to pass it;
- deleting your account.

## You'll hear about it

Changes to the step send you an email, and a notification if you have [notifications](/blog/notifications) on: the step turned on or off, a way added or removed, new backup codes made.

If someone types your correct password and then fails the second step five times in a row, HushOS tells you, at most once an hour. Nobody got in, but your password is known, and that is your cue to change it.

**Account → Two-step sign-in** also lists recent activity: sign-ins that passed the step and how, wrong answers, and changes to the step.

## No phone number needed

HushOS never asks for a phone number, and the step never depends on your inbox. There are no text-message or email codes: every way to pass the step is something you hold.

Your authenticator secret is stored encrypted under a key that hushos.com keeps outside the database, and backup codes are stored only as slow, keyed hashes. The step is a rule our server keeps on top of your password, and your files stay encrypted with keys only your devices hold: [how HushOS protects your files](/security#password) has the details.

## Running your own server?

Two-step sign-in is off on a self-hosted server until you set `TWO_STEP_ENABLED=true`. The rough location in an approval request comes from headers your proxy sets (`TRUSTED_COUNTRY_HEADER`, `TRUSTED_REGION_HEADER`, `TRUSTED_CITY_HEADER`); without them the request names the device and what is asked. The [self-hosting guide](https://github.com/HushOS/HushOS/blob/main/docs/self-hosting.md) has the details.
